# Chapter 5 — General Training Writing Task 1: The Letter
^ Twenty minutes, 150 words, three registers: getting the tone right before getting the words right

General Training candidates write a letter instead of describing data. The task looks easier than the Academic version and is scored on the same four criteria with the same weighting — one third of the Writing band. The trap is register: a letter that is technically correct but tonally wrong loses marks in every criterion at once.

## 5.1 The brief

You are given a situation in three or four lines, followed by **three bullet points** you must address, and an instruction about how to begin. Twenty minutes, at least 150 words.

> *You recently stayed at a hotel and were dissatisfied with the service you received. Write a letter to the hotel manager. In your letter:*
> *&bull; give details of your stay*
> *&bull; explain what went wrong*
> *&bull; say what you would like the manager to do*
> *Begin your letter: Dear Sir or Madam,*

:::key
**All three bullets must be covered, and covered roughly equally.**
Task Achievement is assessed principally on bullet coverage. Two well-developed bullets and one mentioned in passing caps you at Band 6. Give each bullet its own paragraph, and give each paragraph two to four sentences.
:::

## 5.2 Register: the first decision you make

Before you write a single word, decide which of three registers applies. The prompt tells you, usually through the salutation instruction and the relationship described.

| | **Formal** | **Semi-formal** | **Informal** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Recipient** | Someone you don't know, in an official role | Someone you know in a professional or neighbourly context | Friend, family |
| **Prompt signals** | "Dear Sir or Madam"; "the manager"; "the council" | "your landlord"; "your manager, Mr Smith"; "a neighbour" | "your friend"; "Begin: Dear Tom," |
| **Opening** | Dear Sir or Madam, | Dear Mr Hassan, / Dear Dr Lee, | Dear Tom, / Hi Sara, |
| **Closing** | Yours faithfully, | Yours sincerely, | Best wishes, / All the best, / Love, |
| **Contractions** | Never | Sparingly | Freely |
| **Phrasal verbs** | Avoid | Some | Freely |
| **Tone** | Impersonal, restrained | Polite, warm, professional | Chatty, personal |

:::warn
**The faithfully/sincerely rule**
If you begin **"Dear Sir or Madam"** (you do not know the name), you close **"Yours faithfully,"**.
If you begin **"Dear Mr Patel"** (you know the name), you close **"Yours sincerely,"**.
Getting this wrong is a small error that signals to the examiner that you have not learned the conventions. Learn it in thirty seconds and never lose the mark. *Mnemonic: you cannot be sincere with someone whose name you don't know.*
:::

Note also: **"Dear Sir or Madam"** not "Dear Sir/Madam" in careful writing; never "Dear Madam or Sir"; never "To whom it may concern" in IELTS; never "Dear Manager".

## 5.3 The five-paragraph letter architecture

| Paragraph | Content | Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Salutation** | Dear ____, | — |
| **1. Purpose** | Why you are writing, in one or two sentences | 20&ndash;30 |
| **2. Bullet 1** | Background / details | 35&ndash;45 |
| **3. Bullet 2** | The substance | 35&ndash;45 |
| **4. Bullet 3** | The request / outcome | 35&ndash;45 |
| **5. Sign-off line** | A closing sentence, then the complimentary close and a name | 15&ndash;25 |

Total around 180 words, comfortably over the minimum.

**Names.** Sign with a name. For formal letters, invent a plain one — *James Miller*, *Sarah Ahmed*. Do not use your real name if you'd rather not; examiners do not care, but a blank signature looks unfinished. Do not write addresses or dates; they are not required and waste words.

## 5.4 Opening lines by purpose

The first sentence announces the letter's function, and examiners recognise these frames instantly as competent.

### Formal openings

| Purpose | Frame |
| --- | --- |
| Complaint | *I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with...* / *I am writing to complain about...* |
| Request | *I am writing to request...* / *I would be grateful if you could...* |
| Application | *I am writing to apply for the position of... advertised in...* |
| Enquiry | *I am writing to enquire about...* / *I would like to obtain further information regarding...* |
| Information | *I am writing to inform you that...* / *I am writing with regard to...* |
| Resignation | *I am writing to give formal notice of my resignation from...* |
| Apology | *I am writing to apologise for...* |
| Suggestion | *I am writing to propose...* / *I would like to suggest...* |

### Semi-formal and informal openings

| Purpose | Frame |
| --- | --- |
| Thanks | *I just wanted to thank you for...* / *Thanks so much for...* |
| Invitation | *I'm writing to invite you to...* / *How do you fancy...?* |
| News | *I thought I'd drop you a line to let you know...* |
| Apology | *I'm so sorry about...* / *I really must apologise for...* |
| Request (informal) | *I was wondering whether you could...* / *Is there any chance you could...?* |
| Advice | *I hear you're thinking of... — here's what I'd suggest.* |

:::warn
**Never open an informal letter with "I am writing to..."** It is stiff and signals you have memorised one frame for all situations. To a friend: *"It's been ages! I thought I'd write and tell you about..."*
:::

## 5.5 Functional language banks

### Complaining (formal)

- I was extremely disappointed to find that...
- The service fell considerably short of what I had been led to expect.
- On arrival, I discovered that...
- Despite repeated requests, the matter was not resolved.
- This caused me considerable inconvenience.
- I trust you will appreciate my frustration.

### Requesting and demanding redress

| Softness | Frame |
| --- | --- |
| Very polite | *I would be most grateful if you could consider...* |
| Polite | *I would appreciate it if you could...* |
| Neutral | *Could you please...* |
| Firm | *I would like to request a full refund.* |
| Firmer | *I must insist that...* |
| Firmest (still polite) | *I trust that you will take immediate steps to...* |

### Apologising

- Please accept my sincere apologies for...
- I am afraid I will be unable to...
- I do hope this has not caused you any inconvenience.
- I realise this is short notice and I am sorry for the disruption.

### Explaining and giving reasons

- This was due to circumstances beyond my control.
- Owing to an unexpected family commitment, I...
- The reason for this is that...
- As a result of the delay, I was obliged to...

### Making suggestions

- It might be worth considering...
- One possible solution would be to...
- Perhaps you could...
- I would strongly recommend that...

### Closing lines

| Register | Closing sentence |
| --- | --- |
| Formal | *I look forward to receiving your response at your earliest convenience.* |
| Formal | *I would appreciate a reply within fourteen days.* |
| Formal | *Thank you for your attention to this matter.* |
| Semi-formal | *Thank you in advance for your help with this.* |
| Semi-formal | *Please do let me know if you need any further information.* |
| Informal | *Do write back soon and let me know what you think.* |
| Informal | *Can't wait to hear from you!* |
| Informal | *Give my love to everyone at home.* |

## 5.6 Model letters

### Model 1 — Formal complaint (Band 8)

> *You recently bought a piece of equipment for your home but it did not work. Write to the shop. In your letter: describe the item; explain the problem; say what you want the shop to do.*

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with a dishwasher purchased from your Riverside branch on 14 March, and to request that the matter be resolved without further delay.

The appliance in question is a Marlow AquaPro 600, priced at £429, which I selected on the recommendation of one of your sales assistants. It was delivered and installed by your own technicians on 20 March, and I retain both the receipt and the installation certificate.

Unfortunately, the machine has never functioned correctly. From the very first cycle it has failed to drain completely, leaving several centimetres of water in the base, and on two occasions this has overflowed onto the kitchen floor. I reported the fault by telephone on 22 March and again on 29 March, but although I was assured on both occasions that an engineer would be in touch, no one has yet contacted me.

Given that the appliance has been faulty since delivery, I would like to request a full refund rather than a repair. Should you prefer to replace the unit, I would accept an equivalent model of the same value, provided this can be arranged within the next two weeks.

I look forward to receiving your response at your earliest convenience.

Yours faithfully,
James Whitfield

*184 words. Three bullets in three paragraphs. Formal throughout: no contractions, no phrasal verbs, passive constructions, hedged but firm demand.*

### Model 2 — Semi-formal request (Band 7.5)

> *You are studying a course and need to miss two weeks of classes. Write to your tutor. In your letter: explain why you will be absent; say how the absence will affect your studies; suggest how you will catch up.*

Dear Dr Okafor,

I am writing to let you know that I will unfortunately be unable to attend classes between 6 and 20 May, and to ask for your advice about how best to manage the period.

The reason for my absence is that my mother is undergoing a major operation in my home country, and as her only close relative I need to be there both for the surgery itself and for the first stage of her recovery. I appreciate that this is an inconvenient time in the term and I would not be asking if the situation were not serious.

The main difficulty is that I will miss the two seminars on research design as well as the workshop in which the group projects are allocated. I am particularly concerned about the project, since I understand that teams are formed during that session.

To minimise the disruption, I have already arranged for a classmate to record the seminars and share her notes, and I would be very willing to submit an additional written task in place of the workshop if you feel that would be appropriate. I will also have reliable internet access and could join any session remotely if that is possible.

Thank you very much for your understanding.

Yours sincerely,
Amira Haddad

*206 words. Semi-formal: named recipient, warmer tone, some personal explanation, still no contractions.*

### Model 3 — Informal letter (Band 8)

> *A friend is planning to visit your city for a week. Write a letter to your friend. In your letter: say how you feel about the visit; suggest what you could do together; give some practical advice about the trip.*

Dear Tom,

What brilliant news — I got your message about coming over in September and I honestly can't stop grinning. It's been nearly three years since we last saw each other properly, and a whole week is far better than the weekend I was expecting.

I've already started making plans, which you know is dangerous. The obvious thing is to spend a day walking round the old town, because it's exactly the kind of place you'd love: narrow streets, ridiculous amounts of coffee, and a market on Saturdays that sells absolutely everything. If the weather holds, we could also take the ferry across to the islands one day and swim, and I'd really like to drag you up to the observatory in the hills one evening — the view over the whole bay at sunset is genuinely something special.

A couple of practical things. Don't bother booking anywhere to stay, the spare room is yours and I'll be offended if you argue. Do bring something warm for the evenings though; it looks sunny in the photos but it gets surprisingly chilly once the sun goes down. And fly into the eastern airport rather than the main one if you can — it's twenty minutes from my flat instead of an hour and a half.

Let me know your dates as soon as you've booked and I'll take the week off work.

Can't wait!

Love,
Elena

*234 words. Informal: contractions throughout, phrasal verbs, exclamation, direct address, colloquial vocabulary ("brilliant", "drag you up", "can't wait").*

## 5.7 Letter types you should have prepared

| Category | Typical prompts |
| --- | --- |
| **Complaint** | Faulty goods, poor service, noisy neighbours, delayed delivery, damaged accommodation |
| **Request** | Time off, information, a reference, permission, repairs, a refund |
| **Application** | A job, a course, a scholarship, membership, volunteering |
| **Invitation** | A wedding, a party, a visit, a work event |
| **Apology** | Missing an event, breaking something, a cancellation, a mistake at work |
| **Thanks** | Hospitality, a gift, help during difficulty |
| **Information** | Explaining a change of plan, describing an experience, giving directions |
| **Suggestion / proposal** | Improvements to a service, an idea for a local facility, a change at work |
| **Resignation / notification** | Leaving a job, ending a tenancy, cancelling a membership |

Write one of each before your exam. Nine letters, twenty minutes each — three hours of work that covers essentially the entire question bank.

## 5.8 Common failures and their fixes

:::warn
**Ten letter-writing errors**
1. **Register slips** — "I am writing to complain" followed three lines later by "it was a total nightmare". Choose a register and hold it.
2. **Faithfully/sincerely mismatch.** See 5.2.
3. **Ignoring a bullet.** Every bullet gets a paragraph.
4. **Inventing too much detail** and running to 300 words. Invent enough to be specific, no more.
5. **Writing an essay.** No "In conclusion". No "Firstly, secondly, thirdly" as essay signposts.
6. **Headings or bullet points.** Continuous prose only.
7. **No paragraphing.** A wall of text is a Coherence and Cohesion penalty.
8. **Overly aggressive complaint.** "This is disgraceful and I demand compensation" reads as uncontrolled. Formal English complains coldly, not loudly.
9. **Under 150 words.** Count. The penalty is automatic.
10. **Addresses and dates at the top.** Unnecessary, and they inflate your perceived word count while contributing nothing.
:::

## 5.9 Practice tasks

**Task 5A (formal).** You recently attended a training course that was not as described in the brochure. Write to the training provider. Include: details of the course; what was different from the description; what action you want taken. *Begin: Dear Sir or Madam,*

**Task 5B (semi-formal).** You want to organise a small event for colleagues at your workplace. Write to your manager. Include: what the event is; why it would be beneficial; what you need from her. *Begin: Dear Ms Rahman,*

**Task 5C (informal).** A friend has just moved to a new country and is finding it difficult. Write to your friend. Include: how you felt when you heard; your own experience of a similar situation; some advice. *Begin: Dear Anna,*

**Task 5D (formal).** You are dissatisfied with the frequency of buses in your area. Write to the transport authority. Include: which service and why you use it; the problems the current timetable causes; what change you propose.

**Task 5E (semi-formal).** You have been offered a job but need to delay your start date. Write to the person who offered it.

For each: twenty minutes, then check register consistency line by line. Read your letter aloud — register errors are audible long before they are visible.
