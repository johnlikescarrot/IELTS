# Chapter 12: Writing Task 1 — Reports & Letters

## 12.1 Academic Task 1 Essentials

Summarise visual information (graph, table, bar/pie chart, process/cycle, map) in **150+ words (aim 160–180) within 20 minutes**. It is **1/3 of your Writing band**. You write a **report**: objective description + overview. No opinions, no reasons, no explanations from general knowledge.

**The 4-paragraph structure (memorise):**

1. **Introduction (1–2 sentences):** paraphrase the question (*The line graph shows... → The line graph compares/illustrates...*). Never copy the rubric.
2. **Overview (2–3 sentences):** the 2–3 most striking features/trends — NO figures here (or minimal). No overview = Task Achievement capped at Band 5.
3. **Detail paragraph 1:** first group of data with figures (comparisons, highs/lows).
4. **Detail paragraph 2:** second group with figures.

> Video Parts 8–9 teach exactly this: Academic T1 = minimum 150 words, average 160–180, one-third of the mark, a report describing a visual in 20 minutes — scored by the four 25% criteria with Task Achievement first.

## 12.2 The Overview — The Most Important Paragraph

The overview answers: *If I could only tell the reader THREE things, what would they be?* For trend visuals: overall direction + highest/lowest + notable exception. For comparisons: ranking + biggest gap + similarity. For processes: number of stages + start/end points. For maps: biggest changes + overall development.

**Overview formulae (adapt, don't memorise rigidly):**

- *Overall, while A rose steadily throughout the period, B fell sharply before stabilising. C remained by far the highest...*
- *In general, X dominated... whereas Y accounted for the smallest share. The most striking change was...*
- *Overall, the process consists of N stages, beginning with... and ending with...*
- *Overall, the area underwent major development, with the most noticeable changes being...*

>>> Overview drill: for 10 visuals, write ONLY introductions + overviews (5 min each). Show them to a partner: can they sketch the visual's shape from your overview alone? If yes, it works.

## 12.3 The Three Essential Sentences (Video Part 9)

The course's signature Task 1 grammar lesson. Every trend can be expressed three ways — **rotate all three** to demonstrate GRA range:

| # | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | There + be + adjective + noun + in + noun | *There was a gradual rise in the number of arrests in 1952.* |
| 2 | Noun + verb + adverb | *The number of arrests rose gradually.* |
| 3 | Time + saw/experienced/witnessed + adjective + noun + in + noun | *1952 saw a gradual rise in the number of arrests.* |

! Pattern 3 has NO preposition before the time word: *1952 saw...* — never "In 1952 saw...". This single rule fixes one of the commonest Task 1 errors.

**The noun ↔ verb ↔ adjective triangle** (use all three forms across your report):

| Noun | Verb | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|---|
| a rise / an increase | rise / increase | rising / increased | — |
| a fall / a drop / a decline | fall / drop / decline | falling | sharply |
| a fluctuation | fluctuate | fluctuating | wildly |
| a peak | peak / reach a peak | — | — |
| stability | stabilise / remain stable | stable / steady | steadily |
| a surge / a boom | surge / soar / rocket | dramatic | dramatically |

## 12.4 The 12 Trend Phrases (Video Parts 9/11) + Full Toolkit

The course's annotated graph teaches these moves — learn them with the grammar each needs:

1. *rose/increased steadily* (verb + adverb)
2. *rose/increased dramatically* (verb + adverb)
3. *rose/increased gradually* (verb + adverb)
4. *plunged to + figure* (verb + preposition)
5. *peaked at / reached a high of + figure* (verb + preposition)
6. *stood at + figure* (for starting/level points)
7. *fluctuated* (often + *around/between... and...*)
8. *fell/dropped dramatically/sharply* (verb + adverb)
9. *reached a trough of / hit a low of + figure*
10. *remained constant/unchanged/stable at + figure*
11. *levelled off / evened out at + figure*
12. *hit a low of + figure*

**Preposition precision (examiners check these):** *rise TO 50* (endpoint) / *rise BY 20* (change) / *rise FROM 20 TO 50* (range) / *rise OF 20* (amount, noun form: *a rise of 20*) / *stand AT 55* / *peak AT* / *fluctuate BETWEEN X AND Y / AROUND X* / *account FOR 30%* / *represent/make up/constitute + share.*

**Comparisons:** *higher than, twice as high as, by far the highest, the second-highest, marginally above, significantly below, in contrast to, compared with, while/whereas, similarly, likewise, outnumbered, overtook, accounted for the largest share.*

**Approximation (figures are read from visuals — hedge!):** *approximately, roughly, around, about, just over/under, nearly, almost, in the region of.*

## 12.5 FULL WORKED REPORT — Arrests Graph (Video Part 11)

**Task:** *The line graph shows the number of arrests per year for using illegal drugs from 1991 to 2004. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features.*

**Model report (178 words, Band 8–9):**

*The line graph compares yearly arrests for illegal drug use between 1991 and 2004.*

*Overall, arrests fluctuated markedly over the period, with two dramatic peaks around 2000–2001 separated by sharp falls. Despite ending the period at a low, stable level, arrests in the late 1990s were generally higher than in the early 1990s.*

*To begin with, the number of people arrested for handling drugs illegally stood at approximately 55 in 1991. After two years, the figure dropped dramatically to reach 20 in 1993. It then increased gradually from 20 in 1993 to about 25 in 1995, before rising significantly to reach 50 in 1996. Between 1996 and 1999, arrests fluctuated around 40–50 without any clear trend.*

*From 1999, numbers surged to a peak of just over 60 in 2000, but then plunged to a trough of roughly 15 in 2001 — the lowest point in the entire period. Thereafter, arrests remained broadly unchanged at around 15 until 2003, when they edged up slightly to level off at 20 in 2004.*

**Why it scores (annotate your own reports like this):**

- **TA:** introduction paraphrased (*yearly arrests for illegal drug use*); overview = 3 features (fluctuation + two peaks + early/late contrast); all key data selected; 178 words.
- **CC:** 4 paragraphs; overview separated; chronological grouping; linkers (*to begin with, then, before, between, from, but, thereafter, when*); reference (*the figure, it, thereafter*).
- **LR:** stood at, dropped dramatically, increased gradually, fluctuated, surged, plunged, trough, remained unchanged, edged up, levelled off; approximation (approximately, about, just over, roughly, around).
- **GRA:** all three essential patterns (There was... implied; *numbers surged*; *1999...* time-fronted clauses); subordination (*before rising, when they edged up, which...*); passives avoided correctly (active trends).

## 12.6 Comparisons: Tables, Bars, Pies (static visuals)

No time trend → organise by **ranking**, not by table order:

1. Introduction (paraphrase + categories/units).
2. Overview: highest/lowest + most striking contrast/gap.
3. Details A: top group (2–3 items with figures).
4. Details B: bottom group + any exception.

**Static toolkit:** *ranked first/last; dominated by; accounted for X%, double/triple/half the figure for; compared with only X for; by contrast; similarly; the gap between X and Y was striking (60% vs 5%); while X led in..., Y...*

>>> Mini-model (bar chart, urban green space per capita: Vienna 120m², Singapore 66m², Cairo 3m², Mumbai 2m²): "Overall, provision varied enormously: Vienna offered by far the most green space per resident, while Cairo and Mumbai provided negligible amounts. Vienna's 120 square metres per person was almost double Singapore's 66 and roughly forty times Cairo's mere 3. At the bottom of the ranking, Mumbai's 2 square metres was the lowest figure shown, marginally below Cairo." (Overview = variation + top/bottom; details = grouped top vs bottom with multiples — multiples are Band 7+ comparison language.)

## 12.7 Processes & Cycles

- Introduction: *The diagram illustrates the stages in... / how... is produced.*
- Overview: number of stages + start/end (*...comprises eight stages, from... to...*) or cyclical nature (*...is a cyclical process with no fixed starting point*).
- Details: stages in order with sequence linkers (*initially, next, subsequently, following this, once..., after being..., finally*).
- Grammar: **passive voice** for manufactured processes (*The beans are harvested... They are then dried...*); active for natural cycles (*Salmon swim upstream...*).
- Every stage needs its verb: harvesting → drying → roasting → grinding → packaging.

## 12.8 Maps

- Introduction + overview (biggest change + overall trend: urbanisation, modernisation, expansion).
- Details grouped by **location** (north/south; centre/periphery) or **time** (1990 vs 2020, feature by feature).
- Language of change: *was demolished/converted/replaced/expanded to make way for; was built/constructed/developed; remained unchanged; underwent (major/minor) changes; to the north of; adjacent to; in the south-west corner.*
- Tense: past simple for completed change (*The farm was replaced by...*); present perfect/passive for present state (*The area is now dominated by...*).

## 12.9 GT Task 1 — Letters (Video Part 12)

Same scoring, different genre: **write a letter of 150+ words in 20 minutes** for the purpose given. Three tones — matching tone to reader is Task Achievement:

| Tone | Reader | Open / Close | Style markers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formal | stranger, company, official, newspaper | *Dear Sir or Madam, ... Yours faithfully,* / *Dear Mr Ali, ... Yours sincerely,* | No contractions; passive and formal lexis (*I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with...; I would be grateful if...*) |
| Semi-formal | landlord, teacher, colleague you don't know well | *Dear Mr/Mrs + surname ... Yours sincerely / Kind regards,* | Polite but direct; minimal contractions |
| Informal | friend, relative, classmate | *Hi Sara, ... Best wishes / Take care,* | Contractions, phrasal verbs, exclamation (one!), spoken linkers (*Anyway, ...*) |

**Letter structure (4–5 short paragraphs):**

1. Purpose (*I'm writing to... / Just a quick note to...*).
2–4. One paragraph per bullet in the question — cover ALL three bullets (missing a bullet caps TA at Band 5).
5. Closing action (*Let me know... / I look forward to hearing from you.*).

**The 7 letter purposes (with key phrases):**

1. **Complaint:** *I am writing to complain about...; On [date], I...; As a result...; I would appreciate a full refund / replacement / apology.*
2. **Request:** *I would be grateful if you could...; Could you please...?; I was wondering whether...*
3. **Application:** *I am writing to apply for...; I believe I am suitable because...; I have enclosed...*
4. **Apology:** *I am writing to apologise for...; I completely understand that...; To make up for...*
5. **Invitation:** *I'm having... and I'd love you to come; It'll be at... on...; Let me know if you can make it.*
6. **Advice/suggestion:** *I think the best option would be...; If I were you, I'd...; Have you considered...?*
7. **Thank-you:** *Thank you so much for...; It was so kind of you to...; I really appreciated...*

>>> Mini-model (formal complaint, 168 words): "Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to complain about a washing machine I purchased from your City Centre branch on 12 August. The machine, a Model XW-400 costing £429, stopped working after only three weeks of normal domestic use. Specifically, the drum no longer rotates, and water leaks from the base during every cycle. As a result, I have had to use a launderette for the past fortnight at considerable expense. Under your two-year guarantee, I am requesting either a full replacement or a complete refund. I have enclosed copies of the receipt and the guarantee certificate. I would be grateful if you could resolve this matter within fourteen days. I look forward to hearing from you. Yours faithfully, ..." (All bullets: situation + problem + action; formal throughout; no contractions.)

## 12.10 FULL MODEL — Process Report (Coffee Production, 172 words)

*The diagram illustrates the stages involved in producing roasted coffee beans for sale.*

*Overall, the process comprises eight stages, beginning with the harvesting of ripe beans and ending with packaging for retail. The beans undergo two transformations — drying and roasting — separated by several sorting and transport steps.*

*Initially, ripe red beans are picked by hand and then sorted to remove damaged ones. Following this, the good beans are spread in the sun to dry for several days until their moisture content falls. Once dried, they are raked into sacks and transported to the roasting plant.*

*At the plant, the beans are first cleaned mechanically before being roasted at high temperatures, which turns them dark brown and develops their flavour. After cooling, the roasted beans are ground only if required; whole beans skip this stage. Finally, both ground and whole beans are sealed into airtight bags and boxed for distribution to shops.*

*Passive voice throughout (are picked, are spread, are roasted); sequence linkers (initially, following this, once, before, after, finally); overview = stage count + start/end + key transformations.*

## 12.11 FULL MODEL — Map Report (Village 2000 vs 2020, 169 words)

*The maps compare the village of Millfield in 2000 and 2020.*

*Overall, the village underwent major development over the twenty-year period, transforming from a rural settlement into a small commuter town. The most noticeable changes were the construction of a housing estate and a railway link, while the village centre remained largely intact.*

*In 2000, farmland dominated the eastern half of the village, with a single main road running north–south past a church and a handful of cottages. By 2020, the farmland had been replaced by a housing estate of some fifty homes arranged around a cul-de-sac, and a new primary school had been built adjacent to it.*

*To the west, the changes were equally striking. A railway line was constructed along the western edge, with a small station serving the village, and the main road was widened to accommodate commuter traffic. In contrast, the church and the village green in the centre were preserved unchanged, providing continuity with the past.*

*Change language (was replaced, had been built, was constructed, remained, preserved); time contrast (In 2000... By 2020...); grouping by area (east, then west, then centre).*

## 12.12 TWO MORE GT LETTERS (full models)

**Semi-formal request — noisy neighbours (152 words):** "Dear Mr Haddad, I am writing to ask for your help with a noise problem in our building. For the past three weeks, loud music has been playing from Flat 12 every night until after midnight. As our walls are quite thin, my children and I have been unable to sleep properly, and my daughter's schoolwork is beginning to suffer. I have spoken to the residents twice, but unfortunately the situation has not improved. I would be grateful if you, as building manager, could speak to them formally and remind them of the 10 pm quiet-hours rule. If the problem continues, I may need to contact the council's noise team, which I would prefer to avoid. Thank you for your assistance. I look forward to hearing from you. Kind regards, ..." (Purpose → situation → actions taken → requested action → polite close.)

**Informal invitation — dinner party (149 words... extended to 158):** "Hi Sara! Guess what — I've finally moved into the new flat, and I'm having a small dinner party on Saturday to celebrate! It'll be at mine, 14 Rose Street, from 7 pm, and I'm cooking my famous chicken biryani (plus a veggie option, don't worry). Ahmed and Layla are coming too, so it'll be a nice little reunion. Could you bring a dessert? You know your chocolate cake is everyone's favourite! No need to bring anything else — I've got drinks covered. Let me know if you can make it by Thursday so I know how much to cook. Really hope you can come — I haven't seen you in ages! Take care, ..." (Warm open → details → request → close; contractions, phrasal verbs, one exclamation per paragraph max.)

## 12.13 MINI-MODELS — Table + Pie (static comparisons)

**Table (smartphone ownership by age, 2023: 18–29: 98%; 30–49: 94%; 50–64: 79%; 65+: 54%):** "Overall, ownership fell steadily with age: nearly every adult under 30 owned a smartphone, while only just over half of over-65s did. The youngest two groups were almost identical (98% and 94%), but a clear gap opened after 50 — 79% for 50–64s and only 54% for the oldest group, the lowest figure shown." (Overview = age gradient + top/bottom; details = grouped pairs with the gap highlighted.)

**Pie pair (household energy use: 2000 vs 2020 — heating 52%→38%; appliances 18%→31%; hot water 20%→19%; lighting 10%→12%):** "Overall, heating remained the largest use but shrank considerably, while appliances nearly doubled their share. Heating fell from 52% to 38% of household energy, whereas appliances rose from 18% to 31%, overtaking hot water (steady at around a fifth). Lighting grew marginally from 10% to 12%." (Overview = biggest fall + biggest rise; details = paired contrasts with overtake language.)

## 12.14 Task 1 Checking Routine (last 3 minutes)

1. Count words (150+? mark every line's words on paper; computer shows the count).
2. Overview present and figure-light? Introduction paraphrased (no copied chunks)?
3. Every figure accurate? (Misread figures are TA errors.)
4. Tenses: past for past-dated visuals; present for timeless diagrams/maps-present.
5. Articles + prepositions (*an increase in, stood at, accounted for*).
6. No opinion language (*I think, should, good/bad*) in Academic reports.
