# Chapter 10: Speaking Bank — Cue Cards, Questions & Samples

## 10.1 How to Use This Bank

60+ cue cards, 100+ Part 1/3 questions, and 6 full sample talks. Protocol per card: 1-minute plan (Chapter 9.5) → record 2 minutes → transcribe your talk → upgrade 5 phrases → re-record. One card done properly beats ten skimmed.

## 10.2 Part 1 Topic Packs (12 topics × 5 questions + vocabulary)

**1. Hometown:** Where is your hometown? What do you like most about it? Has it changed much in recent years? Would you like to live there in the future? What is it famous for?
*Vocabulary: bustling / laid-back, well-connected, cost of living, landmark, outskirts, densely populated.*

**2. Home/Accommodation:** Do you live in a house or a flat? Which room do you spend most time in? What would you change about your home? Do you plan to move? Who do you live with?
*Vocabulary: spacious / cramped, on the outskirts, landlord, flatmate, cosy, do up (renovate).*

**3. Work:** What do you do? Do you enjoy your job? What are your main responsibilities? Would you like to change jobs? How do you get to work?
*Vocabulary: commute, demanding / rewarding, deadline, colleague, promotion, work-life balance.*

**4. Study:** What are you studying? Why did you choose this subject? What do you like about your course? Are there things you'd change? What are your study plans?
*Vocabulary: major in, assignment, lecture / seminar, campus, curriculum, graduate.*

**5. Hobbies & free time:** What do you do to relax? Have your hobbies changed since childhood? Do you prefer indoor or outdoor activities? Who do you spend free time with? Do you have enough free time?
*Vocabulary: unwind, pastime, keen on, take up, time-consuming, recharge my batteries.*

**6. Food & cooking:** Who cooks in your family? Do you prefer home food or restaurants? Have your eating habits changed? Can you cook a traditional dish? Do you like trying new foods?
*Vocabulary: homemade, balanced diet, recipe, ingredients, foodie, cut down on.*

**7. Sport & health:** Do you play any sports? How do you stay healthy? Did you do sport as a child? Is fitness important in your country? Would you like to try an extreme sport?
*Vocabulary: work out, stamina, team spirit, sedentary, keep fit, adrenaline.*

**8. Travel & holidays:** Do you like travelling? Where did you last go? Do you prefer beaches or cities? Who do you usually travel with? Where would you love to visit?
*Vocabulary: breathtaking, off the beaten track, itinerary, travel companion, jet lag, broaden horizons.*

**9. Technology:** How much time do you spend online? What apps do you use most? Has technology changed your life? Do you prefer phones or computers? Should children have smartphones?
*Vocabulary: addicted to, go viral, user-friendly, glitch, scroll through, digital detox.*

**10. Music & films:** What kind of music do you like? Do you play an instrument? How often do you go to the cinema? Do you prefer films or series? Has your taste changed?
*Vocabulary: catchy, blockbuster, soundtrack, binge-watch, live gig, thought-provoking.*

**11. Friends & family:** How often do you see your family? Do you prefer big or small gatherings? What do you do with friends? Have friendships changed with age? Are you a sociable person?
*Vocabulary: close-knit, get-together, childhood friend, bond, catch up, circle of friends.*

**12. Weather & seasons:** What's the weather like today? Which season do you prefer? Does weather affect your mood? Is climate change noticeable in your country? Do you like rain?
*Vocabulary: scorching / freezing, humid, downpour, mild, unpredictable, gloomy.*

>>> Sample Part 1 answers (tense-matched, Chapter 9.4): "Has your hometown changed much?" → "Yes, enormously. When I was a child it was a quiet town, but over the last decade it has grown into a proper city — new malls, new roads everywhere. The downside is the traffic, which has become terrible." (Present perfect question → present perfect + past contrast.) "Would you like to try an extreme sport?" → "I'd love to try paragliding, actually. I'm not usually adventurous, but a friend did it last year and said the views were unbelievable. I'd need to overcome my fear of heights first, though!" (Conditional → conditional + past anecdote + concession.)

## 10.4 Six Full Sample Talks (Band 7–8 level)

**Talk 1 — Person who taught you something (past-heavy):** "I'd like to talk about my grandfather, who taught me carpentry when I was about ten. He'd been a carpenter all his life, and every summer I'd stay with him in his village. He started me on tiny tasks — sanding wood, holding nails — and by the end of that first summer I could make a simple stool, which I still have. What made him a great teacher was patience: he never touched my work to 'fix' it; he'd just ask, 'What do you think went wrong?' That question taught me more than woodwork — it taught me to solve problems instead of panicking. Looking back, those summers shaped my character. He passed away two years ago, and whenever I repair something in my flat, I feel connected to him. I'd say his real lesson was that making things with your hands gives a satisfaction no office job can match."

**Talk 2 — Quiet place (descriptive):** "My favourite quiet place is the old library in my city centre — a colonial-era building with high ceilings and wooden galleries. I discovered it during exam season last year when my flat was too noisy. Inside, there's a reading room with tall windows overlooking a courtyard garden; the rule is absolute silence, and even the chairs seem designed not to creak. I go most Saturdays now. I usually read for an hour, then just sit and think — something I never do anywhere else. The contrast with the chaotic street outside is almost shocking: traffic and shouting on one side of the door, total calm on the other. It matters to me because it's the only place where my mind genuinely slows down. I hope the council never closes it — in a city that's constantly demolishing old buildings, that library feels like a small act of resistance."

**Talk 3 — Useful app (present-heavy):** "The app I'd choose is a flashcard app I use for vocabulary. I downloaded it about a year ago when I started preparing for IELTS. Basically, you create digital cards with a word on one side and its meaning, example, and pronunciation on the other, and the app schedules reviews using spaced repetition — words you find hard appear daily, easy ones monthly. I use it on the bus, in queues, whenever I have five spare minutes. What I like most is the statistics page: watching my 'mastered' count climb is weirdly motivating. It's genuinely changed my study habits — I used to cram word lists before bed and forget everything; now I retain far more with less effort. The only downside is that it's slightly addictive — I've caught myself reviewing cards at family dinners! I'd recommend it to any language learner."

**Talk 4 — Difficult decision (narrative + reflection):** "The hardest decision I've made was turning down a secure government job last year. I'd passed the exams, my parents were thrilled, and the salary was excellent. But the role was purely administrative, in a city I didn't want to live in, and accepting it meant abandoning my plan to study abroad. I spent two weeks making lists, calling friends, barely sleeping. In the end, I asked myself which choice I'd regret more at forty — and the answer was obvious. My parents were upset for months, though they've come round since I got my IELTS score. That decision taught me that 'safe' and 'right' aren't the same thing. It also made me more decisive generally — now when I face a choice, I use the same forty-year-old test. Sometimes I still wonder 'what if', but I don't regret it."

**Talk 5 — Goal (future-heavy):** "My main goal over the next two years is to complete a master's degree in Canada. I've wanted to study abroad since secondary school, partly for the education and partly to prove to myself I can build a life independently. The plan has three stages: first, achieve band 7.5 in IELTS — I'm sitting the test in three months; second, secure admission and a partial scholarship, which means strong essays and references; third, save enough for the first year's living costs, so I'm working weekends. The biggest obstacle is definitely money, but I've calculated that with part-time work in Canada, it's feasible. Achieving this matters because it would transform my career options back home — international graduates in my field earn roughly double. More than that, though, it's about becoming the person I want to be: self-reliant, open-minded, and genuinely fluent in English."

**Talk 6 — Tradition (culture + opinion):** "I'd like to describe Eid al-Fitr in my country — the festival marking the end of Ramadan. For thirty days, Muslims fast from dawn to sunset, so Eid morning carries this incredible feeling of earned celebration. The day starts with a special prayer at the mosque, shoulder to shoulder with thousands of people, and then families gather for a huge breakfast — sweet dishes especially, after a month without daytime food. Children receive money in decorated envelopes, neighbours exchange plates of food, and the atmosphere is genuinely joyful. What I love most is how it repairs relationships: people visit relatives they haven't spoken to all year, and old quarrels are set aside. Some complain it's becoming commercialised — malls now run 'Eid sales' like Christmas. That's true, but the core remains: gratitude, generosity, and community. I hope we preserve that spirit even as celebrations modernise."

## 10.5 Part 3 Question Sets (with OREC planning notes)

**Set A — Cities (follows hometown/place cards):** Why are more people moving to cities? What problems does urbanisation cause? Should governments limit city growth? How will cities change in the next 20 years?
*OREC notes: opinion (inevitable but manageable) → reasons (jobs, services) → example (your city's growth) → concession (rural decline; remote work may reverse).*

**Set B — Work (follows job/goal cards):** Is job satisfaction more important than salary? Should working hours be reduced? (See the course's automation essay — Chapter 13.) How has technology changed workplaces? Will AI cause mass unemployment?
*OREC notes: balanced view → automation evidence → four-day-week example → wage-protection concession.*

**Set C — Environment (follows nature/place cards):** Who is mainly responsible for pollution — individuals or governments? Are young people more eco-aware? Do small actions (recycling, repair) matter? Should polluting be taxed more?
*OREC notes: shared responsibility → right-to-repair example (Chapter 8!) → systemic-change concession.*

**Set D — Technology (follows app/media cards):** Has social media improved communication? Should children's screen time be limited? What are the dangers of AI? Will physical books disappear?
*OREC notes: nuanced (connection vs attention) → personal example → regulation concession.*

**Set E — Culture (follows tradition/festival cards):** Why should traditions be preserved? Is globalisation destroying local cultures? Should museums be free? How can tourism harm heritage?
*OREC notes: identity value → Eid/commercialisation example → living-culture concession (traditions evolve).*

## 10.7 Three More Sample Talks (Band 8)

**Talk 7 — A machine you use daily (object + habit):** "I'd like to talk about my electric kettle — which sounds ridiculous, but I genuinely use it ten times a day. I'm British-educated in my tea habits: the day starts with builders' tea, strong with milk, and ends with herbal tea. The kettle itself is nothing special — stainless steel, one button — but it structures my whole routine: kettle on means a break is coming; colleagues gather; problems get discussed. During exam season it worked overtime — my flatmate and I measured revision sessions in 'kettles'. I suppose it represents something about my culture: we solve crises with hot drinks. If it broke, I'd replace it within hours — possibly before food. It reminds me that the most important objects aren't impressive; they're the invisible ones holding your day together."

**Talk 8 — A time you learned from a mistake (narrative + lesson):** "Two years ago I missed a flight to Istanbul — a mistake that cost me £300 and taught me more than the trip would have. I'd saved the wrong terminal screenshot, arrived relaxed with forty minutes to spare, and discovered my gate was in another building connected by a train I'd never heard of. I sprinted, pleaded, and watched the plane push back. Stranded overnight, I rebooked, slept on a bench, and finally arrived a day late for my friend's wedding rehearsal — mortified. The lesson stuck permanently: I now screenshot everything twice, arrive absurdly early, and keep offline maps. More deeply, it cured my casual optimism — I plan for things to go wrong. Ironically, I'm now the friend everyone trusts with logistics. Expensive tuition, but worth every penny."

**Talk 9 — A risk worth taking (opinion + story):** "The biggest risk I've taken was quitting a stable job to prepare for studying abroad — which is actually why I'm taking IELTS. I had a decent salary, a clear promotion path, and parents who thought I was insane. The risk was real: eighteen months without income, no guarantee of a visa, and the embarrassment of crawling back if it failed. But staying felt riskier in a different way — waking up at fifty in a life I'd settled for. So I saved for a year, resigned politely, and enrolled in English courses. Fourteen months later: my English has transformed, I've been accepted conditionally by two universities, and — unexpectedly — my old employer offered me freelance work. I'd advise anyone facing a similar choice to distinguish real risks from comfortable fears. Most 'safe' options just hide their costs better."

## 10.9 Part 1 Model Answers — All 12 Topics (Band 7–8)

**Hometown:** "I'm from Izmir, on Turkey's Aegean coast — Turkey's third city, famous for its waterfront promenade called the Kordon. What I love most is the lifestyle: it's relaxed compared to Istanbul, but still big enough for good universities and nightlife." / "It has changed enormously — when I was small it felt like a big town, but endless new apartment blocks have doubled its size. Unfortunately the traffic has become a nightmare."

**Home:** "I live in a two-bedroom flat near the university with my sister. It's nothing fancy, but it's sunny and quiet, which matters more to me than space." / "The room I use most is definitely the kitchen — not just for cooking, but because it's where everyone gathers. My sister and I solve most of our problems over tea there."

**Work:** "I work as a junior accountant at a logistics firm — mainly invoicing and monthly reports. It's not glamorous, but the team is great and I'm learning a lot." / "I commute by metro, about forty minutes door to door. I actually enjoy it — that's when I do my English listening practice."

**Study:** "I'm majoring in nursing — I'm in my second year. I chose it because I want a career that genuinely helps people, and the job security doesn't hurt either." / "The best thing about my course is the practical placements — last semester I spent six weeks in a real hospital ward, which taught me more than any lecture."

**Hobbies:** "My main hobby is photography — street photography, mostly. I go out most weekends with my camera, and I've recently joined a club that exhibits twice a year." / "To unwind on weekdays I just read — usually novels before bed. An hour with a book clears my head better than anything else."

**Food:** "My mother does most of the cooking — classic home food, stews and rice dishes. I can manage a few basics myself, but nothing I'd serve to guests!" / "I've definitely started eating more healthily — I used to live on fast food, but since starting gym I've cut right down and cook most evenings."

**Sport:** "I swim twice a week — it's the only exercise I've ever stuck with. I find running boring, but swimming is almost meditative." / "As a kid I played football every day after school, but I stopped at university. I actually miss the team spirit more than the sport itself."

**Travel:** "My last trip was to Cappadocia — the landscape is unreal, like another planet, especially at sunrise when the balloons go up." / "I usually travel with one close friend — we've learned we have the same pace, which I think is the secret to a good trip. Big groups always end in arguments!"

**Technology:** "Honestly, probably too much — four or five hours a day. Mostly messaging and news, plus English practice apps, which is my excuse!" / "I think children shouldn't have unrestricted smartphones — the content is too much. Limited, supervised access for learning seems the sensible middle ground."

**Music/films:** "I listen to pretty much everything, but mostly indie rock and Turkish folk — my father played folk constantly, so it's nostalgic for me." / "I rarely go to the cinema now — with streaming, I watch series instead. I just finished a brilliant crime drama I basically binge-watched in a week."

**Friends/family:** "I'm very close to my family — we have dinner together every Friday, no exceptions. With cousins and grandparents it's often fifteen people!" / "I prefer small gatherings — three or four friends, good food, real conversation. Big parties exhaust me; I always end up hiding in the kitchen."

**Weather:** "Today it's boiling — typical August, about 35 degrees. I don't mind heat, but the humidity this week has been brutal." / "My favourite season is autumn — the light is beautiful, it's cool enough to walk everywhere, and the city feels alive again after summer."

*Study method: cover the answers, respond to each question aloud, then compare — steal one phrase per answer (famous for, cut right down, the secret to, middle ground, binge-watched) into your active vocabulary.*

## 10.10 Pronunciation Word Lists (course-based + expanded)

**Silent letters (50):** design, resign, resignAtion (note: g sounded!), sign, signature (g sounded!), foreign, reign, daughter, light, night, comb, thumb, lamb, calm, talk, walk, half, castle, listen, fasten, often (t silent in BrE), island, answer, write, wrong, knife, knee, knock, psychology, honest, hour, heir, rhyme, gnome, mnemonic, debt, doubt, subtle, receipt, cupboard,BlackBerry (informal: *sandwich* → "san-widge"), Wednesday (*Wenzday*), February (*Feb-yoo-ary*), vegetable (*veg-tuh-bul*), comfortable (*comf-tuh-bul*), interesting (*in-trest-ing*), chocolate (*choc-lit*).

**Stress pairs (noun ↔ verb):** REcord/reCORD, PREsent/preSENT, IMport/imPORT, EXport/exPORT, CONduct/conDUCT, PROduce/proDUCE, OBject/obJECT, SUBject/subJECT, INcrease/inCREASE, DEcrease/deCREASE.

**Minimal pairs (L1 drills):** p/b: park/bark, cup/cub, rapid/rabid; th/s: think/sink, thick/sick, mouth/mouse; f/v: fan/van, safe/save, leaf/leave; e/i: bed/bid, pen/pin, ten/tin; w/v: west/vest, wine/vine (for German/Slavic speakers); h-dropping: heat/eat, hill/ill (never drop H in English!).

> Practise these lists aloud for 5 minutes before every Speaking session. The course's core message stands: pronunciation is about being *understood with ease* — small, targeted fixes beat accent imitation.
