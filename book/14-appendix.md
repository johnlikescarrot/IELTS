## B. Linking words by function

**Adding:** furthermore, moreover, in addition, additionally, what is more, besides this, likewise, similarly
**Contrasting:** however, nevertheless, nonetheless, conversely, on the other hand, by contrast, that said, even so
**Conceding:** although, even though, while, whereas, admittedly, granted, it is true that, despite, in spite of
**Causing:** because, since, as, owing to, due to, on account of, given that
**Resulting:** therefore, consequently, as a result, thus, hence, accordingly, for this reason
**Exemplifying:** for instance, for example, to illustrate, a case in point, such as, namely, in particular
**Sequencing:** to begin with, firstly, subsequently, following this, thereafter, finally
**Emphasising:** indeed, notably, crucially, above all, in particular, it should be stressed that
**Qualifying:** to some extent, in certain respects, arguably, broadly speaking, by and large
**Concluding:** in conclusion, to conclude, in summary, on balance, all things considered, ultimately

## F. Letter phrases (General Training Task 1)

**Formal openings:** I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with... / to request... / to enquire about... / to apply for... / with regard to...
**Informal openings:** I thought I'd drop you a line to... / It's been ages! / I'm writing to invite you to...
**Complaining:** I was extremely disappointed to find that... / fell considerably short of / caused me considerable inconvenience
**Requesting:** I would be grateful if you could... / I would appreciate it if... / I must insist that...
**Apologising:** Please accept my sincere apologies for... / I am afraid I will be unable to...
**Formal closings:** I look forward to receiving your response at your earliest convenience. / Thank you for your attention to this matter.
**Informal closings:** Do write back soon. / Can't wait to hear from you! / Give my love to everyone.
**Sign-offs:** Dear Sir or Madam &rarr; **Yours faithfully** | Dear Mr/Ms Name &rarr; **Yours sincerely** | Dear [first name] &rarr; **Best wishes / All the best / Love**

## G. Essay frames (Writing Task 2)

## O. Word-stress quick reference

*-tion, -sion, -cian, -ity, -ical, -ular, -ic:* stress the syllable **before** the suffix
*-ee, -eer, -ese, -ette:* stress the **suffix**
Two-syllable **nouns/adjectives:** stress 1 | Two-syllable **verbs:** stress 2
**Noun/verb pairs:** **RE**cord (n) / re**CORD** (v); **IN**crease / in**CREASE**; **PRE**sent / pre**SENT**
**Compound nouns:** stress the first element (**AIR**port) | **Compound adjectives:** stress the second (old-**FASH**ioned)

## R. A closing note

This book was assembled from twelve hours of recorded live teaching — a real classroom, with real students making real mistakes and being corrected in real time. That origin shows in its priorities. It spends more time on word limits than on rare vocabulary; more on the overview paragraph than on stylistic flourish; more on not giving short answers than on sounding native.

That is the right emphasis. The candidates who improve fastest are almost never the ones who learn the most English between attempts. They are the ones who stop throwing marks away: who count the words, who write the overview, who keep listening after the first plausible answer, who give a reason with every answer, who finish passage three.

None of that requires talent. All of it requires practice, honestly timed and honestly reviewed.

Good luck.
