# Chapter 5: Listening Practice Sets & Tapescripts

## 5.1 How to Use These Practice Sets

You need audio to practise Listening authentically. Use these sets in three ways: (1) read the tapescript aloud (or ask a partner/app to read it) while you answer — this trains prediction and spelling; (2) use the questions as prediction drills (classify every gap without audio); (3) study the tapescript paraphrase notes after attempting official audio practice. Sets A and B are complete with questions, tapescripts, keys, and error analysis. Set C is a full 40-question mock framework with scripts.

> For audio-accompanied practice, pair this chapter with official IELTS sample recordings: the techniques are identical, and Chapters 3–4 taught you to exploit them.

## 5.2 Practice Set A — Section 1: Community Sports Centre Membership

### Questions 1–10

*Write no more than TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer.*

**Riverside Community Sports Centre — Membership Application**

Example: Enquiry taken by: *Sarah*

- Name of applicant: 1 ...
- Address: Flat 4, 2 ... Road, Exeter
- Contact number: 3 ...
- Date of birth: 4 ... March
- Membership type: 5 ...
- Preferred activities: swimming and 6 ...
- Health note: mild allergy to 7 ...
- Induction session: 8 ... at 6 pm
- Monthly fee: maximum 9 ... pounds
- Payment method: 10 ...

### Tapescript A

*SARAH: Good morning, Riverside Sports Centre. How can I help?*

*MAN: Morning. I'd like to join the gym, please. Can I register over the phone?*

*SARAH: Of course. Can I take your name first?*

*MAN: Yes — it's David Thornton. That's T-H-O-R-N-T-O-N.*

*SARAH: Thornton... And your address, David?*

*MAN: Flat 4, 52 Mulberry Road, Exeter. M-U-L-B-E-double R-Y.*

*SARAH: Got it. And a contact number?*

*MAN: My mobile is 0770... sorry, 0770 946 331. So that's O-double-7-O, 9-4-6, double-3-1.*

*SARAH: Thanks. Date of birth?*

*MAN: The 18th of March, 1998.*

*SARAH: Now, which membership type are you interested in? We have off-peak, standard, and premium.*

*MAN: Hmm. I work shifts, so I can only come during the day. Off-peak sounds fine — actually, wait. My shifts change next month. Let me take standard to be safe.*

*SARAH: Standard it is. Which activities will you mainly use?*

*MAN: Swimming for sure, and the climbing wall. I've never tried it but it looks fun.*

*SARAH: Great. Any medical conditions we should note?*

*MAN: Only a mild allergy to chlorine dust... no, sorry — to chalk dust. The stuff climbers use. It makes me sneeze.*

*SARAH: I'll note "chalk dust". Now, new members attend an induction — we have one on Thursday at 6 pm, or Saturday at 10.*

*MAN: Thursday's no good... Saturday morning then. At 10?*

*SARAH: Hmm, actually the Saturday one is fully booked. We could do Friday at 6 pm instead?*

*MAN: Friday at 6 is fine.*

*SARAH: The standard fee is £34 a month, but there's a joining offer — maximum £28 for the first six months.*

*MAN: £28 — that's good. How do I pay?*

*SARAH: Direct debit monthly, or card each visit. Most members choose direct debit.*

*MAN: Direct debit then, please.*

### Key & analysis A

| Q | Answer | Trap / skill tested |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Thornton | Spelled surname — copy letters exactly (THORNTON). |
| 2 | Mulberry | Spelled street name + double-R; number 52 is printed, not the answer. |
| 3 | 0770946331 | O-double-7-O...: write digits; "double-3" = 33. |
| 4 | 18th | Ordinal + printed month; year 1998 is extra. |
| 5 | standard | Correction trap: off-peak considered, standard chosen. |
| 6 | climbing wall | Two words, within limit; "swimming" is printed. |
| 7 | chalk dust | Correction trap: chlorine → chalk. |
| 8 | Friday | Rejected options: Thursday (caller refuses), Saturday (full), Friday confirmed. |
| 9 | 28 | Maximum price: 34 standard vs 28 offer — question asks maximum. |
| 10 | direct debit | Two words; "card" is the rejected alternative. |

**Paraphrase log (add to your notebook):** *register = join; contact number = mobile; mainly use = which activities; medical conditions = health note; attend = come to; fee = pay; each visit = per session.*

## 5.3 Practice Set B — Section 3: Tutorial Discussion

### Questions 21–26

*Choose the correct letter, A, B, or C.*

21. The students chose traditional markets as their presentation topic because
- A. they are disappearing rapidly.
- B. they combine history and economics.
- C. the tutor suggested the topic.

22. Maria thinks the questionnaire results were disappointing because
- A. too few people responded.
- B. the questions were unclear.
- C. the answers were too similar.

23. The tutor warns that the population statistics may be
- A. out of date.
- B. difficult to verify.
- C. irrelevant to the topic.

24. Both students agree that the market visit
- A. took too long to organise.
- B. produced better material than the library.
- C. should have lasted a full day.

### Questions 27–30

*Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS for each answer.*

**Presentation plan**

- Opening: define 27 ...
- Main part 1: compare prices of 28 ...
- Main part 2: interview with a 29 ...
- Closing: recommend 30 ... for the market's future

### Tapescript B (extract)

*TUTOR: So, your presentation is on traditional markets. How did you settle on that?*

*MARIA: Well, you gave us a free choice... we wanted something with historical background but also a modern business angle. Markets gave us both.*

*TUTOR: Good reasoning. And the questionnaire — 200 sent out, yes?*

*JAMES: Only 60 came back. The response was poor — but honestly, the bigger problem was the questions themselves. Question 4 confused everyone; half the answers made no sense.*

*MARIA: True. Though even the clear questions got almost identical answers, which wasn't very useful either.*

*TUTOR: A common problem. Now, your population figures — the 2019 census?*

*JAMES: Yes, showing a 12% rise near the market district.*

*TUTOR: Treat those with caution — the district boundaries changed in 2021, so the numbers may not be strictly comparable. Verify against the council's revised tables.*

*MARIA: Will do. The best part was definitely the field visit — two hours at the market gave us more than a week in the library.*

*JAMES: Absolutely. Though I wish we'd stayed the whole day — the afternoon traders never got interviewed.*

*MARIA: Hmm, we had enough for ten minutes, honestly.*

*TUTOR: Right — how will you structure it?*

*JAMES: We'll open by defining our key term — "informal economy" — then compare the prices of fresh produce with supermarket prices, then play the interview with a stallholder...*

*MARIA: ...Mrs Adeyemi, the fabric seller. And we'll close by recommending guided tours as a way to secure the market's future.*

### Key & analysis B

| Q | Answer | Why (and the distractor) |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | B | History + economics ("historical background but also a modern business angle"). A never mentioned; C contradicts "free choice". |
| 22 | B | "the questions themselves... Question 4 confused everyone". A (60/200) is true but Maria names unclear questions as the disappointment; C is her secondary remark. |
| 23 | A | Boundaries changed → figures may be out of date / not comparable. |
| 24 | B | "more than a week in the library" — agreed. A and C each raised by one speaker only. |
| 27 | informal economy | Opening definition; two words. |
| 28 | fresh produce | Compare prices of... (vs supermarket prices). |
| 29 | stallholder | The interviewee's role (fabric seller = stallholder). |
| 30 | guided tours | Recommendation for the future. |

## 5.5 Practice Set D — Section 2: Newtown Library Tour (Map + MC)

### Questions 11–14 — Completion

*Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer.*

- The library was built in 11 ...
- Late returns cost 12 ... per day
- The café is on the 13 ...
- Free computer courses run on 14 ...

### Questions 15–20 — Map labelling

*Label the map. Choose SIX answers from the box.*

| Box: A fiction hall / B children's corner / C study desks / D local history archive / E café / F computer room / G issue desk / H reading garden |

- 15 ... (ground floor, left of entrance)
- 16 ... (ground floor, opposite entrance)
- 17 ... (first floor, above the entrance)
- 18 ... (first floor, far end from stairs)
- 19 ... (outside, behind the building)
- 20 ... (basement, down the stairs)

### Tapescript D

*GUIDE: Welcome to Newtown Library! A quick orientation. The building opened in 2011 — purpose-built, all on three levels plus a garden. First, practicalities: late returns are 30 pence per item per day, so watch your dates. The café — great coffee — is on the top floor with the views. And our free computer courses for beginners run every Tuesday morning — just sign up at the desk.*

*Now the layout. Ground floor: as you come in the main entrance, the issue desk is on your left — that's where you borrow and return. Directly opposite the entrance, through the glass doors, is the fiction hall — novels, obviously, plus audiobooks. Upstairs on the first floor: right above the entrance, overlooking the street, you'll find the study desks — silent zone, no phones. At the far end of the first floor, past the newspapers, is the local history archive — Victorians, railways, all that. Outside, behind the building, is the reading garden — lovely in summer. And downstairs in the basement: the children's corner — story time Saturdays — and next to it the computer room. Oh — the café I mentioned is also up top, beside the study desks... no wait, above them, on the second floor. Sorry — top floor, second floor, same thing here!*

### Key D

11 2011, 12 30 pence, 13 top floor (accept: second floor), 14 Tuesday (morning); 15 G, 16 A, 17 C, 18 D, 19 H, 20 B. (F computer room and E café are decoys: café location is described but not asked as a map gap; computer room is mentioned beside the answer but is not itself a gap.)

*Traps: 13 — "top floor" vs "second floor" (same thing — either accepted); café decoy (described twice, never a gap — do not force it); 20 — basement has TWO rooms, only the children's corner is gapped.*

## 5.6 Practice Set E — Section 4: Lecture — Sleep and Memory

### Questions 31–40 — Note completion

*Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS for each answer.*

**Sleep and Memory**

- Old belief: the brain 31 ... during sleep
- New finding: the brain replays experiences in 32 ...
- Key process: memory 33 ...
- Transfer: from the hippocampus to the 34 ...
- Deep sleep occurs in the 35 ... of the night
- Experiment: volunteers learned sequences of 36 ...
- Improvement without practice: 37 ... faster
- REM sleep handles 38 ... memories
- Students sleeping under 7 hours lost 39 ... per subject
- Worst strategy: 40 ...

### Tapescript E (condensed lecture)

*"For decades, sleep was regarded as passive — the brain simply switching off. We now know the sleeping brain replays the day in fast-forward, consolidating memory. The key process, memory consolidation, moves fragile memories from the hippocampus to the neocortex for long-term storage. This happens mainly in deep slow-wave sleep, in the first half of the night. In our experiment, volunteers learned sequences of finger movements; those with 90 minutes of deep sleep performed 24% faster next morning — improvement without practice. A second stage, REM sleep, processes emotional memories, stripping their painful charge. Among 8,000 school students, those sleeping under seven hours scored half a grade less per subject — they retained less despite equal study. The worst strategy of all? The all-night revision session — like writing an essay, then deleting the file."*

### Key E

31 switches off (accept: switching off), 32 fast-forward, 33 consolidation, 34 neocortex, 35 first half, 36 finger movements, 37 24% (accept: twenty-four percent), 38 emotional, 39 half a grade, 40 all-night revision (accept: all-nighter / all-night revision session — within limit: "all-night revision").

*Note: this lecture mirrors Chapter 8's Sleep passage — the same content tested receptively (Reading) and aurally (Listening). Study them together: vocabulary learned once, used twice.*

## 5.8 Tapescript Study Method (all sets)

1. Attempt under time, transfer, mark with the key (Chapter 15 consolidates all keys).
2. For every error, find the exact tapescript lines and highlight: the **paraphrase** (what hid the answer) and the **distractor** (what misled you).
3. Dictate: replay/read the 10 seconds around each error and transcribe perfectly.
4. Re-attempt the same set after 7 days — target: 100%.

> One set studied this deeply teaches more than five sets merely scored. The video course models this exactly: the lecturer replays, annotates synonyms on screen, and drills the same exercise until the class masters it.
