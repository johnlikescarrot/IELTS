# Chapter 8 — Reading the Band Descriptors Like an Examiner
^ What the marker is actually looking at when your script is in front of them

Writing and Speaking are marked against published criteria. Most candidates have never read them. This chapter reproduces their substance and, more usefully, translates each one into a concrete instruction about what to do differently.

## 8.1 Writing Task 1 — Task Achievement

| Band | What the descriptor says | What it means for you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **5** | Generally addresses the task but format may be inappropriate; **recounts detail mechanically with no clear overview**; may be inaccurate | You listed every data point. You wrote no overview. **Fix: write the overview, and select.** |
| **6** | Addresses the requirements; presents an overview with information appropriately selected; some details may be inaccurately reported | You have an overview but it is thin, or you misread a figure. **Fix: check every number; make the overview state 2&ndash;3 patterns.** |
| **7** | Covers the requirements; presents a **clear overview of main trends, differences or stages**; clearly presents and highlights key features but could be more fully extended | Your overview works. **Fix: extend the body — add comparisons and consequences of the data pattern.** |
| **8** | Covers all requirements sufficiently; presents, highlights and illustrates key features **clearly and appropriately** | Selection is precise, comparisons explicit, figures well chosen. |

:::key
**The word "overview" appears in every band above 5 and is absent from Band 5.** That is the clearest signal in the entire descriptor set. No overview, no Band 6.
:::

## 8.2 Writing Task 2 — Task Response

| Band | Descriptor substance | Your action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **5** | Addresses the task only partially; format may be inappropriate; expresses a position but development is unclear; some main ideas but limited/irrelevant/repetitive | You answered part of the question, or you listed ideas without developing them. **Fix: underline every part of the task; develop two ideas fully rather than four thinly.** |
| **6** | Addresses all parts, though some more fully than others; presents a relevant position although conclusions may become unclear or repetitive; presents relevant main ideas but some inadequately developed | **Fix: equalise paragraph development; make sure your conclusion restates rather than repeats.** |
| **7** | Addresses all parts; presents a **clear position throughout**; presents, extends and supports main ideas, though there may be a tendency to over-generalise or supporting ideas may lack focus | **Fix: replace general claims with specific mechanisms and examples.** |
| **8** | Sufficiently addresses all parts; presents a **well-developed response with relevant, extended and supported ideas** | Every paragraph has a mechanism, an example and a consequence. |

## 8.3 Coherence and Cohesion (both tasks)

| Band | Descriptor substance | Your action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **5** | Some organisation but no clear progression; inadequate, inaccurate or **over-use of cohesive devices**; may be repetitive; may not write in paragraphs or use them inadequately | **Fix: paragraph properly; cut half your linkers.** |
| **6** | Arranges information coherently with clear overall progression; uses cohesive devices effectively but cohesion within/between sentences may be faulty or **mechanical**; may not always use referencing clearly | **Fix: vary linker position; use reference words (this, such, the latter) instead of another "Furthermore".** |
| **7** | Logically organises information with clear progression; uses a **range of cohesive devices appropriately** though there may be some under-/over-use; presents a clear central topic within each paragraph | **Fix: ensure every paragraph has one controlling idea stated in its topic sentence.** |
| **8** | Sequences information and ideas logically; manages all aspects of cohesion well; uses paragraphing sufficiently and appropriately | Cohesion is invisible — it reads naturally. |

:::warn
**"Over-use of cohesive devices" appears at Band 5.** Candidates who have been told "use linking words" often use one per sentence and score *lower* for it. Cohesion that draws attention to itself is mechanical cohesion.
:::

## 8.4 Lexical Resource

| Band | Descriptor substance | Your action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **5** | Limited range, minimally adequate; noticeable errors in spelling/word formation that may cause difficulty | **Fix: build topic vocabulary sets; check word forms.** |
| **6** | Adequate range for the task; attempts less common vocabulary with some inaccuracy; some errors in spelling/word formation but they do not impede communication | **Fix: learn collocations, not isolated words.** |
| **7** | Sufficient range to allow **flexibility and precision**; uses less common lexical items with some awareness of style and collocation; may produce occasional errors | **Fix: focus on precision — the exact right word, not the rarest one.** |
| **8** | **Wide** resource, fluently and flexibly conveying precise meanings; skilful use of uncommon items though occasional inaccuracies; rare errors in spelling/word formation | Idiom used naturally; register consistently controlled. |

:::key
**"Flexibility and precision" at Band 7 — not rarity.**
Using *ameliorate* where *improve* was needed is not Band 7 vocabulary; it is a collocation error. Using *mitigate* correctly with *risk*, *impact* or *effect* is. Learn words inside their collocations or do not use them.
:::

## 8.5 Grammatical Range and Accuracy

| Band | Descriptor substance | Your action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **5** | Limited range; attempts complex sentences but these are less accurate than simple ones; **frequent** grammatical errors that may cause some difficulty | **Fix: master two complex structures properly before attempting more.** |
| **6** | **Mix of simple and complex** forms; some errors in grammar and punctuation but they rarely reduce communication | **Fix: increase the proportion of complex sentences; check articles and agreement.** |
| **7** | A **variety** of complex structures; **majority of sentences error-free**; good control of grammar and punctuation with a few errors | **Fix: proofread for the recurring error — usually articles, plurals or subject-verb agreement.** |
| **8** | **Wide range** of structures; the **majority of sentences are error-free**; only occasional errors or inappropriacies | Relative clauses, conditionals, participles, inversions all used naturally. |

The two halves — range and accuracy — are equally weighted and pull against each other. Candidates aiming for range make more errors; candidates aiming for accuracy write simply. Band 7 requires you to attempt complexity **and** control it, which is why Chapter 9 focuses on a small number of structures practised to automaticity.

## 8.6 Speaking descriptors

### Fluency and Coherence

| Band | Substance |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Usually maintains flow but uses repetition, self-correction and slow speech to keep going; may over-use simple connectives; some breakdown in coherence |
| 6 | Willing to speak at length though may lose coherence through hesitation, repetition or self-correction; uses a range of connectives but not always appropriately |
| 7 | Speaks at length **without noticeable effort**; some language-related hesitation or repetition; uses a range of connectives and discourse markers with some flexibility |
| 8 | Speaks fluently with only occasional repetition or self-correction; **hesitation is usually content-related**; develops topics coherently and appropriately |

### Lexical Resource (Speaking)

| Band | Substance |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Manages to talk about familiar and unfamiliar topics but uses vocabulary with limited flexibility; attempts paraphrase with mixed success |
| 6 | Has a wide enough vocabulary to discuss topics at length and make meaning clear despite inappropriacies; **generally paraphrases successfully** |
| 7 | Uses vocabulary resource flexibly to discuss a variety of topics; **uses some less common and idiomatic vocabulary** with some awareness of style and collocation; uses paraphrase effectively |
| 8 | Uses a wide resource readily and flexibly to convey precise meaning; uses less common and idiomatic vocabulary **skilfully**; uses paraphrase effectively as required |

Note that **idiomatic language** appears explicitly at Band 7 for Speaking, whereas idioms are discouraged in Writing. The registers differ. *"It's not really my cup of tea"* is a Band 7 signal in Speaking and a register error in a Task 2 essay.

### Grammatical Range and Accuracy (Speaking)

| Band | Substance |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Produces basic sentence forms with reasonable accuracy; limited range of complex structures, usually containing errors |
| 6 | Uses a mix of simple and complex structures with limited flexibility; may make frequent mistakes in complex structures though these rarely cause comprehension problems |
| 7 | Uses a range of complex structures with some flexibility; **frequently produces error-free sentences** though some grammatical mistakes persist |
| 8 | Uses a wide range of structures flexibly; **majority of sentences error-free** with only occasional inappropriacies or basic/non-systematic errors |

### Pronunciation

| Band | Substance |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Uses a limited range of pronunciation features with mixed control; shows some effective use but this is not sustained; can generally be understood though mispronunciation reduces clarity at times |
| 6 | Uses a range of pronunciation features with mixed control; shows some effective use but with lapses; **generally understood throughout**, though mispronunciation of individual words or sounds reduces clarity at times |
| 7 | Shows all the positive features of Band 6 and some of Band 8 |
| 8 | Uses a **wide range** of pronunciation features; sustains flexible use with only occasional lapses; **easy to understand throughout**; L1 accent has minimal effect on intelligibility |

:::key
**Band 8 says "L1 accent has minimal effect on intelligibility" — not "no accent".**
Having a first-language accent is compatible with Band 9. Having word stress in the wrong place, or dropping consonant clusters so that words become unrecognisable, is not.
:::

## 8.7 Self-assessment: marking your own work honestly

Marking your own writing is difficult because you know what you meant. Three techniques that make it possible:

**1. Mark for one criterion at a time.** Read your essay four times, looking only for Task Response, then only for Coherence, then Lexis, then Grammar. Mixed-criterion reading produces an overall impression, which is exactly what you cannot trust.

**2. Use the descriptor language as questions.**
- Is there a *clear position throughout*? Point to the sentences that establish it.
- Is each idea *extended and supported*? Point to the mechanism and the example.
- Are the *majority of sentences error-free*? Count them. Literally count.

**3. Leave it 24 hours.** You cannot proofread your own prose on the day you wrote it.

### The error log

Keep one from today. Three columns: **the error**, **the correction**, **the rule**.

| Error | Correction | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "The people is happy" | "The people are happy" | *people* is plural |
| "informations" | "information" | uncountable |
| "discuss about" | "discuss" | transitive, no preposition |
| "In nowadays" | "Nowadays" | no preposition |
| "He suggested me to go" | "He suggested that I go" | *suggest* + that-clause |

After four weeks you will find that 80% of your errors are five or six recurring patterns. Fixing six patterns is achievable; fixing "my grammar" is not.
