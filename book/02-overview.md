# Chapter 1 — The Test, The Scoring, and the Strategy Behind Both
^ What you are actually being measured on, and how the numbers work

Before any technique is useful, you need an accurate mental model of the machine you are feeding. Most candidates carry a vague one, and the vagueness costs them half a band. This chapter builds a precise one.

## 1.1 The two versions of IELTS

IELTS comes in two forms. **Academic** is for university entry and professional registration. **General Training** is for migration, secondary education and work experience. They are not two different tests; they are one test with two of its four components partially swapped.

| Component | Academic | General Training | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Listening | Identical | Identical | 30 min + 10 min transfer (paper) |
| Reading | 3 long academic passages, 40 questions | 5 shorter texts: social survival, workplace, one long general-interest passage, 40 questions | 60 min, no extra transfer time |
| Writing Task 1 | Describe visual data: graph, chart, table, process, map | Write a letter: formal, semi-formal or informal | 20 min recommended |
| Writing Task 2 | Discursive essay | Discursive essay (slightly simpler topics) | 40 min recommended |
| Speaking | Identical | Identical | 11&ndash;14 min |

Two consequences follow immediately, and the course stresses both.

First, **Listening and Speaking are shared**, so roughly half of everything in this book applies to every candidate regardless of version. Second, **General Training Reading is not "easier" — it is differently hard.** The texts are simpler, but the pass mark is higher. The same raw score buys you a lower band.

## 1.2 The band scale, and what the raw scores buy

Each of the four components is scored from 0 to 9 in half bands. The **Overall Band Score** is the mean of the four, rounded to the nearest half band, with .25 rounding up and .75 rounding up.

:::key
**Rounding, concretely**
6.0 + 6.0 + 6.5 + 6.5 = 25.0 &divide; 4 = **6.25 &rarr; rounds up to 6.5**
6.0 + 6.0 + 6.0 + 6.5 = 24.5 &divide; 4 = **6.125 &rarr; rounds down to 6.0**
6.5 + 6.5 + 6.0 + 7.0 = 26.0 &divide; 4 = **6.5 exactly**
This is why half a band in your weakest component is often worth more than a full band in your strongest.
:::

### Writing and Speaking: criterion-based

There is no raw score. Four named criteria, each contributing **25%** of the component band, are scored 0&ndash;9 and averaged.

| | Writing Task 1 & 2 | Speaking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criterion 1 | Task Achievement (T1) / Task Response (T2) | Fluency and Coherence |
| Criterion 2 | Coherence and Cohesion | Lexical Resource |
| Criterion 3 | Lexical Resource | Grammatical Range and Accuracy |
| Criterion 4 | Grammatical Range and Accuracy | Pronunciation |

And one detail that changes how you spend your fifty minutes of writing:

:::key
**Task 2 is worth twice Task 1**
The Writing band is calculated as (Task 1 &times; 1 + Task 2 &times; 2) &divide; 3. As the teacher says repeatedly, Task 1 is "one third of the whole mark." A brilliant Task 1 cannot rescue a thin Task 2. Write Task 1 in twenty minutes and get out.
:::

Worked example: Task 1 scores 6.0, Task 2 scores 7.0. Writing band = (6 + 14) &divide; 3 = 6.67 &rarr; **6.5**. Reverse it — Task 1 at 7.0, Task 2 at 6.0 — and you get (7 + 12) &divide; 3 = 6.33 &rarr; **6.5** as well, but you were one mark from 6.0. The asymmetry always favours protecting Task 2.

## 1.3 The shape of the test day

Listening, Reading and Writing are taken back to back with no breaks, in that order, totalling two hours and forty minutes. Speaking may be on the same day or up to seven days before or after.

:::warn
**The transfer-time trap**
In the **paper-based** test you get ten extra minutes after Listening to copy answers onto the answer sheet. In **Reading you do not.** Your answers must be on the answer sheet within the sixty minutes. In the **computer-based** test there is no transfer time for Listening either — you type directly, with two minutes at the end to check. Know which test you are taking, and practise in that format.
:::

## 1.4 Where the marks actually leak

Across twelve hours of live teaching and student correction, the same failure modes appear again and again. They are worth naming now, because each chapter of this book is in some sense a response to one of them.

| Leak | Component | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exceeding the word limit | Listening, Reading | 2, 3 |
| Writing the right idea but misspelling it | Listening | 2 |
| Answering from knowledge instead of the text | Reading (T/F/NG) | 3 |
| Reading every word instead of scanning | Reading | 3 |
| No overview paragraph | Writing Task 1 | 4 |
| Describing every data point instead of selecting | Writing Task 1 | 4 |
| No clear position; essay drifts | Writing Task 2 | 6 |
| One-sentence, unsupported answers | Speaking | 7 |
| Memorised speeches that don't answer the question | Speaking | 7 |
| Simple sentences only, no subordination | Writing, Speaking | 9 |
| Repeating the prompt's vocabulary verbatim | Writing, Speaking | 10 |
| Mispronunciation causing genuine misunderstanding | Speaking | 11 |

## 1.5 A diagnostic to run before you read on

Do this honestly, this week, before you study anything. You need a baseline.

1. Take one **full Listening test** under exam conditions, one play only. Mark it. Record the raw score and, crucially, categorise every error: *didn't hear it*, *heard it but spelled it wrong*, *broke the word limit*, *lost my place*.
2. Take one **full Reading test** in sixty minutes with no extensions. Mark it. Note how many questions you left blank because you ran out of time.
3. Write **one Task 2 essay in forty minutes**, handwritten if you are taking the paper test. Count the words. Count the paragraphs.
4. Record yourself answering **eight Part 1 Speaking questions** and one cue card. Listen back with a stopwatch: how long is your average Part 1 answer? If it is under fifteen seconds, that alone is costing you a band.

Write the four results on the inside cover of this book. In eight weeks, repeat the diagnostic. The comparison is the only honest feedback you have.

:::key
**The strategic conclusion of this chapter**
Your overall band is the *mean* of four numbers. The fastest route upward is almost never "improve my English." It is: find the lowest of the four numbers, identify which specific leak in the table above is producing it, and close that leak. Most candidates can gain a full band in eight weeks without their underlying English improving at all — purely by no longer throwing marks away.
:::
