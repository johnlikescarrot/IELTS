# Chapter 6: Reading Mastery — Skills & Method

## 6.1 The Reading Test in Detail

60 minutes, 3 sections, 40 questions — and **no transfer time**. Everything (reading, answering, checking) happens inside the hour, so time management is a scored skill in everything but name.

| Feature | Academic | General Training |
|---|---|---|
| Section 1 | Long text (~700–900 words), easiest of the three | 2–3 short everyday texts (adverts, notices, timetables) |
| Section 2 | Long text, medium | 2 workplace texts (handbooks, procedures, benefits) |
| Section 3 | Long text, hardest (dense argument/discussion) | 1 long general-interest text |
| Total words | 2,200–2,750 | ~2,000–2,750 |
| Time target | 15 / 18 / 22 min + 5 min checking | 12 / 15 / 25 min + 8 min checking |

> Video Parts 3–4 teach Reading exactly as this chapter presents it: the test examines your *skills*, not your knowledge; it is "really a test of 2 things" (finding information + understanding it); and at its core, "IELTS Reading = a test of understanding SYNONYM LANGUAGE."

## 6.2 The Two Things Reading Tests

The course's framing (Part 3) is the best mental model ever given to beginners:

1. **Locating:** finding the 2–3 sentences that contain the answer (keywords → synonyms → location).
2. **Understanding:** comparing the question's claim with the passage's meaning closely enough to judge match, contradiction, or absence.

Most candidates fail at step 1 (they can't find it) or rush step 2 (they match words, not meaning). Every technique below serves one of these two steps.

## 6.3 The Three Reading Speeds

| Speed | Name | What you do | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fast | Skimming | First + last sentence of each paragraph; titles, names, numbers, italics | First 90 seconds per passage: map the territory |
| Faster | Scanning | Eyes hunt one specific thing (a date, name, number, keyword) | Completion, short-answer, and locator questions |
| Slow | Reading for detail | Read 2–4 sentences word-by-word, parsing grammar | TFNG/YNNQ, multiple choice, matching opinions |

**The 90-second survey (do this for every passage):**

1. Read the title and rubrics (10 sec).
2. First sentence of every paragraph (40 sec) — number the paragraphs.
3. Note names, dates, numbers, technical terms and *where* they sit (20 sec).
4. Read the question set's first lines; decide your question order (20 sec).

! Never "just start reading". Candidates who read the whole passage first run out of time; candidates who survey first finish early. The survey feels slow; it is fast.

## 6.4 Synonym Language — The Heart of the Test

Questions paraphrase; passages state. Your job is meaning-matching:

| Passage says | Question says |
|---|---|
| *people came from far and wide to see the marvel* | *people were drawn from all over to see the tunnel* |
| *many believed it was the Eighth Wonder of the World* | *it was the Eighth Wonder of the World* (claim strengthened → False) |
| *the aim had been for the tunnel to be used by wheeled vehicles to transport cargo so that it could bring in a profit* | *the aim was to turn a profit as a tourist attraction* (purpose changed → False) |
| *the tunnel became part of the London Underground railway system which continues to be its use today* | *it is no longer used as a pedestrian walkway* (True — function changed) |
| *valued mostly for its vivid colour* | *prized chiefly for its bright hue* |

The Thames Tunnel exercise (Video Parts 1/4–5) annotates these links in colour: green for True (meaning matches), pink for False (contradiction), yellow for Not Given (absent). Adopt the same three-colour habit with highlighters.

**The 60 most-recycled paraphrase patterns** (learn these; they appear in every test):

- cause: *lead to, give rise to, trigger, be responsible for, stem from, be attributed to*
- increase: *rise, grow, climb, soar, surge, expand, on the up*
- decrease: *fall, drop, decline, plummet, shrink, dwindle, on the wane*
- important: *crucial, vital, key, major, significant, paramount*
- show: *demonstrate, reveal, indicate, illustrate, highlight, suggest*
- people: *individuals, the public, residents, citizens, the population*
- think: *believe, consider, argue, maintain, claim, hold the view*
- many: *numerous, a wide range of, a host of, myriad, prevalent*
- change: *shift, transform, alter, adapt, evolve, convert*
- problem: *issue, challenge, drawback, obstacle, hurdle, setback*

> Every Reading error in your log should end with a synonym pair. After 10 passages you will own 100+ pairs — and the test will start repeating them back to you.

## 6.5 Keywords: Finding the Answer Location

**Step 1 — pick 2–3 keywords per question.** Best keywords are rare and hard to paraphrase: names (*Thames*), numbers (*1843*, *£500,000*), dates (*1865*), technical terms (*subaqueous*). Weak keywords are common words (*tunnel, people, first*) — usable only in combination.

**Step 2 — predict paraphrases.** Before scanning, brainstorm: *first → earliest, initial, pioneering; built → constructed; under a river → subaqueous, beneath the Thames.*

**Step 3 — scan, then slow down.** Find the keyword zone, then read 2 sentences before and after at detail speed. The answer is almost always within that window.

**Step 4 — match meaning, not words.** Ask: does the passage *say the same thing* (True), *contradict it* (False), or *not mention it* (Not Given)?

>>> Thames Q1 walkthrough: "The Thames Tunnel was the first tunnel ever built under a river." Keywords: first, tunnel, under a river. Scan finds: "It was the first subaqueous tunnel ever built." Paraphrase check: first = first; tunnel = tunnel; under a river = subaqueous (under water). Meaning matches → True. Note the method: keyword → locate → paraphrase → judge. Every question in this book follows it.

## 6.6 Time Management: The 15-18-22 System (Academic)

| Passage | Read + answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (easiest) | 15 minutes | Bank time; these marks are cheapest |
| 2 | 18 minutes | Steady pace |
| 3 (hardest) | 22 minutes | Needs the most detail-reading |
| Buffer | 5 minutes | Transfer check (paper), guesses, review flagged items |

Rules:

1. **One minute per question, maximum.** Past 90 seconds with no answer: guess, mark, move on.
2. **Do not do 1–40 in order.** Within each passage, answer in this order: completion → short answer → TFNG/YNNQ → multiple choice → matching → headings last (headings are fastest once you know the passage).
3. **Completion answers come in passage order** — use answered neighbours to bracket missing ones.
4. **Keep a 5-minute buffer.** A rushed final passage costs more than a careful first one gains.

For GT: Sections 1–2 are fast (12 + 15 min) — Section 3 deserves 25 minutes.

## 6.7 Answer Discipline

- Same word-limit strictness as Listening: *NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS* means two words.
- Copy words **exactly** as spelled in the passage (British/American as printed).
- TFNG vs YNNQ: T/F = facts in the passage; Y/N = the *writer's opinions/claims*. Same technique, different rubric — read it every time.
- For letter choices (A–E, i–vii), write the letter clearly; on paper, capitals.
- Never blank: eliminated to 50/50? Guess between the two. No idea? Guess the least-chosen letter so far.

## 6.9 The 10 Most-Tested Reading Traps

1. Word-matching without meaning-matching (same words, different claim).
2. Strengthened/weakened claims (*believed to be → was*; *some → all*; *may → will*).
3. Purpose/cause swaps (profit *from cargo* vs profit *as a tourist attraction*).
4. Time traps (*is used today as...* vs *was built for...*).
5. Not Given vs False (contradicted vs absent — the costliest confusion; see Chapter 7).
6. Heading "detail traps" (a heading matching one example sentence, not the paragraph's main idea).
7. List-order assumptions (matching/headings are NOT in order — only completion is).
8. Spending 5 minutes on one 1-mark question.
9. Copying three words for a two-word gap.
10. Changing correct answers in the last minute without evidence (first instinct wins ~70% of the time).
