# Chapter 2: Study Plans, Diagnostics & Self-Assessment

## 2.1 Start Here: The 30-Minute Diagnostic

Before you study a single strategy, measure where you stand. This diagnostic needs no special materials — use the practice sets in Chapters 5, 8, 10, and 12–13, or any official sample test.

1. **Listening (10 min):** Attempt one Section 1 + one Section 3 set (20 questions). Record raw score /20 and error types (spelling? plural? distractors? numbers?).
2. **Reading (10 min):** Attempt one full passage (13–14 questions) in 20 minutes... then stop at 10 minutes and mark what you finished. Record completed/total and correct/total. This exposes your *speed gap*.
3. **Writing (5 min planning only):** Read one Task 2 question and write only the introduction + essay plan. Check: clear position? paraphrased question? two distinct body ideas?
4. **Speaking (5 min):** Record yourself answering three Part 1 questions and one Part 2 cue card (1 minute). Listen back for: tense errors? repetition? silence longer than 3 seconds?

Enter the results in the tracker at the end of this chapter. Repeat the same diagnostic every 3–4 weeks: improvement in *error types* matters more than raw scores.

> Video: the course opens (Part 1) by teaching you to classify questions before answering them. Diagnosis works the same way: classify your weaknesses first, then train them deliberately.

## 2.2 Setting Target Bands

Work backwards from your requirement:

| Requirement | Typical profile | Priority modules |
|---|---|---|
| Overall 6.0, no minimums | 6-6-6-6 or 6.5-6-6-5.5 | Push strongest receptive skill (Listening/Reading) to 6.5+ to bank points. |
| Overall 6.5, min 6.0 each | 7-6.5-6.5-6 or similar | No module below 6.0: fix your weakest module first. |
| Overall 7.0, min 6.5 each | 7.5-7-7-6.5 | Productive skills (Writing/Speaking) usually cap the score: 50% of study time there. |
| 7.0+ in every module | 7.5-7-7-7.5 | Precision phase: error elimination, advanced cohesion, idiomatic range. See Chapter 14. |
| Overall 7.5–8.0 | 8-8-7.5-7.5 | Near-native consistency: timed practice, examiner-level self-correction, 5,000+ word active vocabulary. |

! Be honest about your starting level. Moving one full band typically needs 150–200 guided study hours. A beginner at 4.5 aiming for 7.0 should plan 6–12 months; this book's 12-week plan assumes roughly one band of progress with 2 hours of daily study.

## 2.5 The 7-Day Emergency Plan

Damage control only — it cannot build language, but it can add 0.5 band through technique.

1. **Day 1:** Listening Sections 1–2 method + 4 practice sets. Memorise the number/date/spelling rules (Chapter 3).
2. **Day 2:** Reading: T/F/NG + completion method + 2 passages. Learn the skimming routine (Chapter 6).
3. **Day 3:** Speaking Parts 1–2: prepare 10 topics + 6 cue-card stories you can adapt (Chapter 10).
4. **Day 4:** Task 1: memorise the overview formula + 3 sentence patterns + 12 trend phrases (Chapter 12). Write 2 reports.
5. **Day 5:** Task 2: memorise 2 essay structures + 20 cohesive devices (Chapter 13). Write 2 essays.
6. **Day 6:** Full Listening + Reading mock under time. Correct, log errors, re-drill weakest type.
7. **Day 7:** Light review of error log + vocabulary notebook. Sleep 8 hours. Prepare documents and route.

! In the final 48 hours, never learn new strategies — only consolidate. New techniques under stress cause more errors than they fix.

## 2.6 How to Practise Each Skill

### Listening practice protocol

1. Read the questions first and **predict**: number? name (spelling)? date? noun type?
2. Listen once without pausing (exam realism). Write answers directly.
3. Listen again with the tapescript (all practice sets in Chapter 5 include scripts): mark every paraphrase and distractor you missed.
4. Dictate trouble spots: replay 5-second segments and transcribe word-for-word until perfect.
5. Log errors by type, not just count: *distractor / spelling / plural / word-limit / lost-place*.

### Reading practice protocol

1. Spend 90 seconds on survey: title, first lines, question types, then choose your question order (Chapter 6).
2. Answer in 17 minutes per passage (leaving 9 minutes for transfer/checking on paper; 3 minutes spare on computer).
3. Correct with the key, then for every error write the **synonym pair** that hid the answer (e.g., *drawn from all over = came from far and wide*).
4. Re-read the passage aloud the next day for vocabulary; add 5–10 items to your notebook.

### Writing practice protocol

1. Plan every task on paper (T1: 3 min; T2: 5 min) — never skip planning, even in practice.
2. Write to time (T1: 17 min; T2: 35 min) with the last minutes reserved for checking.
3. Self-correct against the 4 criteria checklists (Chapter 11) before looking at model answers.
4. Rewrite one paragraph to band-9 standard. Rewriting teaches more than writing new essays.
5. Get human feedback at least twice before the exam (teacher, tutor, or exchange partner).

### Speaking practice protocol

1. Record everything. A 2-minute recording reviewed for 10 minutes beats 30 minutes of unreviewed talking.
2. Score each recording against the 4 criteria (Chapter 9): give yourself 5–9 per criterion with one evidence note each.
3. Fix one criterion per week (e.g., Week 5: tenses; Week 6: linking; Week 7: less-common vocabulary).
4. Shadow native speech 10 minutes daily: play a 20-second clip, repeat simultaneously, matching rhythm and stress.

## 2.7 Sample Daily Schedules (2-hour day)

**Listening day:** 0:00–0:10 vocab (20 number/spelling drills) → 0:10–0:25 method review (one Chapter 3 section) → 0:25–0:55 timed set (one full section) → 0:55–1:15 tapescript study (paraphrase log) → 1:15–1:30 dictation of trouble spots → 1:30–1:50 second set → 1:50–2:00 error log.

**Reading day:** 0:00–0:15 synonym review (flashcards) → 0:15–0:35 one passage timed (20 min) → 0:35–0:55 correction + synonym log → 0:55–1:15 second passage, different type → 1:15–1:35 correction → 1:35–1:55 re-read + vocabulary mining → 1:55–2:00 plan next session.

**Writing day:** 0:00–0:20 analyse + plan two tasks (no writing!) → 0:20–0:40 Task 1 timed → 0:40–1:15 Task 2 timed → 1:15–1:40 self-mark vs checklists → 1:40–2:00 rewrite weakest paragraph to perfection.

**Speaking day:** 0:00–0:15 pronunciation drills (Chapter 10.9 lists) → 0:15–0:35 Part 1: 10 questions recorded → 0:35–1:00 Part 2: two cards (plan + record each) → 1:00–1:25 Part 3: one set, OREC → 1:25–1:50 transcribe + upgrade + re-record best/worst → 1:50–2:00 score vs criteria.

## 2.8 Breaking Plateaus (when scores stall 3+ weeks)

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Prescription |
|---|---|---|
| Listening stuck at 6.0 | Distractors + spelling, not comprehension | 1 week: distractor drills only (Ch 4.10) + daily dictation |
| Reading stuck at 6.0 | Time, not English (unfinished Q36–40) | 2 weeks: 17-min passages; easy-first order enforced with timer |
| Writing stuck at 6.0–6.5 | Error density + generic ideas | Rewrite protocol: 1 old essay/week to perfection; 5 new collocations forced per essay |
| Speaking stuck at 6.0–6.5 | One weak criterion (usually GRA tenses or LR repetition) | Transcribe 3 recordings; fix ONLY the weakest criterion for 2 weeks |
| Everything stuck | Passive study (reading about English, not using it) | Flip ratio to 80% production (writing/speaking/writing) + error log |

> Plateaus are information, not failure: they name your next syllabus. The candidates who break through are the ones who change the training, not the ones who repeat it louder.
