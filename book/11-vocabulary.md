# Chapter 10 — Vocabulary, Collocation and Paraphrase
^ Precision over rarity: building a lexical resource that actually raises bands

The course returns to one word more often than almost any other: **paraphrase**. It is the mechanism of Reading, the mechanism of Listening, the requirement of every Writing introduction and the marker of Band 7 in Speaking. This chapter treats paraphrase as a skill to be built, and the vocabulary around it as a tool kit.

## 10.1 What Lexical Resource actually measures

Four things, in descending order of importance for most candidates:

1. **Precision** — the exact word for the meaning.
2. **Collocation** — words used with their natural partners.
3. **Appropriacy** — the right register for the task.
4. **Range** — variety, including some less common items.

Note that range is fourth. Candidates almost always attack it first, and that is why so many essays contain expensive words used wrongly.

:::warn
**The rare-word trap.** A Band 6 candidate writes: *"A plethora of individuals espouse the notion that ubiquitous technology engenders deleterious ramifications."*
Every word is "advanced". The sentence is unreadable, two collocations are wrong, and the register is grotesque. It scores **lower** than: *"Many people believe that the spread of technology has harmful effects."*
The descriptor says "flexibility and precision", not "obscurity".
:::

## 10.2 Collocation: the real unit of vocabulary

Words travel in groups. Learning a word without its partners produces sentences that are technically correct and audibly wrong.

| Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- |
| make a research | **conduct/do** research |
| do a mistake | **make** a mistake |
| say a lie | **tell** a lie |
| strong rain | **heavy** rain |
| big problem (weak) | **serious/major/pressing** problem |
| high experience | **extensive/considerable** experience |
| take a decision (BrE ok) / do a decision | **make** a decision |
| gain weight ✓ / win weight ✗ | **gain/put on** weight |
| pay attention on | pay attention **to** |
| discuss about | **discuss** |
| reach to a conclusion | **reach** a conclusion |
| fulfil the requirements ✓ | — |

## 10.3 Upgrading basic vocabulary

The five words that most damage Speaking and Writing bands: **good, bad, thing, very, a lot of.**

### Instead of *good*

| Context | Better |
| --- | --- |
| a good idea | a sound / sensible / constructive idea |
| a good result | a favourable / positive / encouraging result |
| a good teacher | an effective / dedicated / inspiring teacher |
| a good film | an outstanding / compelling / absorbing film |
| good weather | fine / pleasant / glorious weather |
| a good argument | a persuasive / cogent / compelling argument |
| a good relationship | a close / harmonious / strong relationship |

### Instead of *bad*

| Context | Better |
| --- | --- |
| a bad effect | a detrimental / adverse / harmful effect |
| a bad decision | a misguided / ill-advised / questionable decision |
| a bad situation | a dire / troubling / precarious situation |
| bad behaviour | disruptive / inappropriate / antisocial behaviour |
| a bad experience | an unpleasant / distressing / frustrating experience |
| bad weather | severe / inclement / atrocious weather |

### Instead of *very + adjective* — use one strong word

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| very big | enormous, vast, substantial, considerable |
| very small | tiny, minute, negligible, marginal |
| very important | crucial, vital, essential, paramount |
| very difficult | demanding, arduous, formidable, challenging |
| very good | excellent, outstanding, exceptional |
| very bad | appalling, dreadful, disastrous |
| very tired | exhausted, drained |
| very interested | fascinated, captivated |
| very hot | scorching, sweltering |
| very cold | freezing, bitter |
| very happy | delighted, thrilled, elated |
| very angry | furious, irate, incensed |
| very common | prevalent, widespread, ubiquitous |
| very expensive | exorbitant, prohibitive |

### Instead of *a lot of / many*

*a considerable number of, a substantial proportion of, numerous, a great deal of (uncountable), a wealth of, countless, the vast majority of, an abundance of*

### Instead of *thing*

Be specific: *factor, aspect, element, issue, feature, consideration, phenomenon, development, matter, respect.*

## 10.4 The mechanics of paraphrase

Five techniques. Use two or three in combination for a strong paraphrase; one alone is usually too close to the original.

### 1. Synonym substitution

*The government should invest more in public transport.*
&rarr; *The authorities ought to allocate greater funding to public transit.*

Beware: synonyms are rarely exact. *Thin/slim/skinny/slender* differ in connotation. *Cheap/inexpensive/affordable* differ in register.

### 2. Word-class change (nominalisation and its reverse)

*Governments decided quickly.* &rarr; *The speed of the government's decision...*
*The population increased rapidly.* &rarr; *There was a rapid increase in the population.*

This is the most powerful technique, because it forces the whole sentence to be rebuilt.

| Verb | Noun | Adjective | Adverb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| analyse | analysis | analytical | analytically |
| benefit | benefit | beneficial | beneficially |
| compete | competition | competitive | competitively |
| decide | decision | decisive | decisively |
| economise | economy | economic/economical | economically |
| educate | education | educational | educationally |
| environment | environment | environmental | environmentally |
| grow | growth | growing | — |
| industrialise | industry | industrial | industrially |
| maintain | maintenance | — | — |
| respond | response | responsive | responsively |
| signify | significance | significant | significantly |
| succeed | success | successful | successfully |
| technology | technology | technological | technologically |

:::warn
**Word-form errors are marked at every band.** *"He was success"*, *"an economic problem"* when you meant *economical*, *"he responded responsibly"* when you meant *responsively*. Learn the whole family, not one member.
:::

### 3. Voice change

*Researchers conducted the study.* &rarr; *The study was conducted by researchers.*

### 4. Negation of the opposite

*Few people supported the plan.* &rarr; *The plan was not widely supported.*
*It is uncommon.* &rarr; *It rarely occurs.*
*He rejected the offer.* &rarr; *He did not accept the offer.*

### 5. Structural reordering

*Because costs rose, demand fell.* &rarr; *The fall in demand was a consequence of rising costs.*
*Although it was expensive, it worked.* &rarr; *Its high cost notwithstanding, it proved effective.*

### Paraphrase practice

Take this sentence and produce four versions:

> **Original:** "Many young people today prefer to communicate online rather than face to face."

1. *A large proportion of the younger generation now favours digital interaction over meeting in person.* (synonym + word class)
2. *Online communication is preferred by many of today's youth to in-person contact.* (voice)
3. *A significant number of young people are less inclined to interact face to face than they are to communicate through digital channels.* (negation of opposite)
4. *For much of the younger generation, the default mode of interaction has shifted from physical presence to online exchange.* (structural)

Do this with ten sentences a day for three weeks.

## 10.5 Topic vocabulary banks

Task 2 and Speaking Part 3 draw from a limited set of domains. Build twenty to thirty items for each.

### Education

*curriculum, syllabus, pedagogy, rote learning, critical thinking, tuition fees, scholarship, vocational training, higher education, literacy, numeracy, dropout rate, academic attainment, streaming, extracurricular, lifelong learning, distance learning, teacher-pupil ratio, standardised testing, continuous assessment*

**Phrases:** *broaden one's horizons; instil discipline; foster independent thought; bridge the attainment gap; equip students with skills; a well-rounded education*

### Environment

*carbon footprint, emissions, renewable energy, fossil fuels, biodiversity, deforestation, habitat loss, conservation, sustainability, recycling, landfill, greenhouse gases, global warming, ecosystem, pollutant, single-use plastic, carbon neutral, offsetting*

**Phrases:** *mitigate climate change; reduce our environmental impact; adopt greener practices; a finite resource; irreversible damage; at the expense of the environment*

### Technology

*automation, artificial intelligence, algorithm, digital divide, connectivity, data privacy, surveillance, cybercrime, innovation, obsolescence, screen time, misinformation, digital literacy, remote working, e-commerce*

**Phrases:** *keep pace with technological change; at the click of a button; revolutionise the way we work; raise concerns about privacy; a double-edged sword (Speaking only)*

### Health

*obesity, sedentary lifestyle, preventive medicine, public healthcare, life expectancy, chronic illness, mental wellbeing, nutrition, epidemic, immunisation, healthcare provision, waiting times*

**Phrases:** *lead a sedentary lifestyle; place a strain on health services; adopt a balanced diet; the stigma surrounding mental illness*

### Work and economy

### Society and government

### Culture and media

Task 1 and Task 2 require formal written English. Speaking allows — and at Band 7 rewards — informality and idiom.

| Informal | Formal |
| --- | --- |
| get | obtain, acquire, receive |
| get worse | deteriorate, worsen |
| get better | improve, recover |
| find out | discover, ascertain, determine |
| put off | postpone, defer |
| set up | establish, found |
| look into | investigate, examine |
| bring about | cause, produce, generate |
| go up / go down | rise / fall, increase / decrease |
| deal with | address, tackle, handle |
| think about | consider, contemplate |
| a lot of | a great deal of, numerous |
| kids | children, young people |
| stuff / things | items, materials, factors |
| big | significant, considerable |
| sort of / kind of | somewhat, to some extent |
| show | demonstrate, illustrate, indicate |
| help | assist, facilitate, support |
| use | employ, utilise, make use of |
| start | commence, initiate, embark on |
| end | conclude, terminate, cease |
| need | require, necessitate |
| make sure | ensure, guarantee |

:::warn
**Register rules for Writing Task 1 and 2**
✗ No contractions: write *do not*, not *don't*.
✗ No phrasal verbs where a single verb exists.
✗ No idioms: *"at the end of the day"*, *"the tip of the iceberg"*, *"a piece of cake"*.
✗ No slang, no exclamation marks, no "etc.", no "&".
✗ No direct address: *"As you can see..."*, *"Let me tell you..."*
✗ No rhetorical questions as a structural device.
✓ Impersonal constructions: *"It could be argued that..."*, *"There is evidence to suggest..."*
:::

And the inverse, for Speaking:

:::key
**Idiom is a Band 7 marker in Speaking.**
*It's not really my cup of tea. / I was over the moon. / It's a bit of a mixed blessing. / That's easier said than done. / I'd take it with a pinch of salt. / It's second nature to me now. / We see eye to eye on most things. / It cost an arm and a leg.*
Use two or three across the test, naturally. A string of idioms crammed in sounds rehearsed and scores worse than none.
:::

## 10.7 How to actually learn vocabulary

:::key
**Five principles**
**1. Learn in chunks, not single words.** Store *"pose a serious threat to"*, not *"pose"*.
**2. Learn the whole word family.** *analyse / analysis / analytical / analytically*.
**3. Learn with a sentence you wrote yourself.** A word in your own sentence about your own life is retained; a word in a list is not.
**4. Space the repetition.** Review at 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 3 weeks. Use flashcards or an app that does this for you.
**5. Force production.** Reading a word teaches recognition; writing and saying it teaches use. IELTS Writing and Speaking test production.
:::

**A realistic weekly routine:** 15 new chunks per week, actively used in writing or speech within 48 hours, reviewed at spaced intervals. Sixty chunks a month, 180 over a twelve-week preparation — far more useful than 1,000 words seen once.

### The word list to avoid

Lists of "Band 9 vocabulary" circulating online typically contain words like *plethora, myriad, paradigm, quintessential, ubiquitous*. These are not band-raisers. They are the words examiners see in every memorised script, usually miscollocated. Prefer accurate, well-collocated mid-frequency academic vocabulary — *substantial, address, mitigate, widespread, compelling, viable* — used correctly.
