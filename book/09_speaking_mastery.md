# Chapter 9: Speaking Mastery — Criteria & Method

## 9.1 The Speaking Test in Detail

11–14 minutes with a human examiner (face-to-face or video call), recorded for quality control. Identical for Academic and General Training. Three parts, one continuous conversation:

| Part | Time | Format | You should produce |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Interview | 4–5 min | 8–12 questions on 2–3 familiar topics (home, work, study, hobbies, food, sport...) | 2–4 sentences per answer: direct answer + reason/detail + example |
| 2 — Long turn | 3–4 min (1 min prep + 1–2 min talk) | Cue card: describe X. *You should say...* (3–4 bullets) *...and explain...* | 1.5–2 min structured monologue covering all bullets |
| 3 — Discussion | 4–5 min | 4–8 abstract questions linked to Part 2's theme | Extended answers: opinion + reasons + example + concession/alternative |

> Video Parts 5–7 teach Speaking to beginners through the four assessment criteria, verb-tense matching, all three parts, and pronunciation (including first-language interference). This chapter expands those lessons into a complete method; Chapter 10 gives you the question bank.

## 9.2 The Four Criteria (25% Each)

Examiners score four independent criteria. A weakness in any one caps your band — balance wins.

### Criterion 1: Fluency and Coherence (FC)

- **Fluency:** speak at a natural pace with minimal hesitation. Occasional pausing to find ideas is fine; constant pausing to find *language* is penalised. Self-correction is fine in moderation ("Sorry — I meant...").
- **Coherence:** connect ideas logically: *because, so, but, however, for example, firstly... finally, in other words.* Long turns need visible structure (past → present → future; opinion → reason → example).
- Band 7 requires: stretches of speech without noticeable effort + some less common connectives (*whereas, as a result, on the other hand*) with only occasional misuse.

### Criterion 2: Lexical Resource (LR)

- **Range:** variety and appropriacy — topic vocabulary (*commute, landlord* for home; *deadline, colleague* for work), collocations (*make progress, take responsibility*), less common items (*bustling, nostalgic, overwhelming*).
- **Accuracy:** precision — the right word for the meaning (*slim* vs *skinny* vs *underweight* carry different attitudes).
- **Paraphrase:** talking around gaps ("*the machine that washes clothes — a washing machine*"). The course stresses this explicitly: circumlocution is a *scored skill*, not a failure.
- Band 7 requires: some less common vocabulary with awareness of style and collocation + occasional errors only.

### Criterion 3: Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA)

- **Range:** mix simple, compound, and complex sentences: conditionals (*If I had more time, I'd...*), relatives (*...which is why...*), concession (*Although it's crowded, I love it*), passives, perfect tenses, reported speech.
- **Accuracy:** error density matters — Band 7 tolerates "frequent error-free sentences"; Band 6 shows errors that rarely block meaning.
- The #1 GRA booster for beginners: **answer in the examiner's tense** (see 9.4).

### Criterion 4: Pronunciation (P)

The course teaches this from the official descriptors (Part 6): pronunciation = the ability to speak clearly in order to be understood. It includes:

- **Listener effort:** how hard the examiner works to follow you.
- **Unintelligible stretches:** amounts of speech the listener cannot understand (must be rare at Band 7).
- **L1 interference:** first-language effects on sounds (p/b, th/s), word stress (*PHOtograph* vs *phoTOgraphy*), and sentence rhythm.
- **Features that raise bands:** clear individual sounds, correct word stress, natural sentence stress (emphasis on content words), and intonation that signals meaning (lists, questions, surprise).

> You do NOT need a British or American accent. Any accent is accepted if it is clear. The course contrasts BE/AE only to show both are fine — clarity, not origin, is scored.

## 9.3 Part 1 Method: Answer + Extend (Never One Word, Never a Speech)

**The 3-step answer:** Direct answer → reason or detail → example or contrast. 15–25 seconds per question.

>>> Examiner: "Do you like your hometown?" Weak: "Yes, I like it very much." (10 words, no extension — Band 5.) Strong: "Yes, I love it, mainly because it's small enough to feel friendly but big enough to have everything I need. For example, I can walk to work in fifteen minutes, whereas in the capital my friends commute for hours." (Tense: present throughout; linker: mainly because, for example, whereas; collocation: commute; contrast structure.)

**Topic preparation (not memorisation):** Prepare *ideas + vocabulary* for 12 core topics (Chapter 10), never full scripts — memorised answers are detectable (flat intonation, wrong question fit) and penalised under FC.

**If you don't understand:** "Sorry, could you repeat that?" / "Do you mean...?" — asking for clarification is natural and unpenalised (once or twice).

## 9.4 Tense Matching — The Beginner's Superpower

The course's signature Speaking lesson (Part 5, *Using the right verb tense*): **notice the examiner's tense and answer in the same tense.** Most Part 1 questions telegraph their tense:

| Examiner's question | Tense signal | Your answer's tense |
|---|---|---|
| *Where do you live?* | do (present simple) | Present simple: *I live...* |
| *What did you do last weekend?* | did (past) | Past simple: *I visited...* |
| *Have you ever tried...?* | have (present perfect) | Present perfect → past: *Yes, I've tried... I went...* |
| *Are you planning to...? / Will you...?* | planning/will (future) | Future: *I'm going to... / I'll...* |
| *How has your city changed?* | has changed (present perfect) | Present perfect + past contrast: *It has grown... Ten years ago it was...* |
| *Would you like to...?* | would (conditional) | Conditional: *Yes, I'd love to...* |

**Drill:** take 30 Part 1 questions; label each question's tense before answering. Within a week, tense-matching becomes automatic — and your GRA band jumps half a band with no new grammar learned.

! Mixed time references need mixed tenses: "I *live* in Jeddah now, but I *grew up* in a village, and I*'ve lived* in the city for six years." Examiners reward exactly this control.

## 9.5 Part 2 Method: The 1-Minute Plan + 2-Minute Talk

**Cue-card anatomy** (e.g., the course's *Describe your favourite hobby*): topic + 3–4 *You should say* bullets + *and explain* (opinion/feeling — the most important bullet for LR/GRA range).

**The 60-second plan (write 8–12 words, not sentences):**

| Seconds | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Choose your story (familiar > impressive — a true simple story beats a false grand one) |
| 10–40 | One keyword per bullet + one linking phrase each (*At first... / The reason... / What I love most...*) |
| 40–60 | Plan your ending (feeling/opinion/future — never trail off) |

**The talk structure (PAST–PRESENT–FUTURE fits most cards):**

1. **Open (10 sec):** "I'm going to talk about... which..." (paraphrase the card — instant LR credit).
2. **Bullets 1–2 (40 sec):** facts in the past (*I started... / My uncle taught me...*).
3. **Bullets 3–4 (40 sec):** present habits + benefits (*Now I... / It helps me...*).
4. **Explain (20 sec):** feeling/opinion + future (*...means a lot to me because... / I hope to...*).

>>> Mini-model (hobby card, ~200 words / 1:45): "I'm going to talk about photography, which has been my favourite hobby for about three years. I first got into it when my uncle gave me his old camera for my birthday — at first I just took random pictures of my family, but I quickly became fascinated by light and composition. These days I go out almost every weekend, usually to the old city, where I photograph street life and architecture. What I enjoy most is how it forces me to slow down and really observe things other people walk past. It's also surprisingly social: I joined a club, and we've exhibited our work twice. The main benefit for me is stress relief — after a week of classes, an afternoon with my camera completely clears my head. This hobby matters to me because it changed how I see ordinary places; a dull street can become beautiful through a lens. In the future I'd love to travel with my camera — Japan in cherry-blossom season is the dream." (Past → present → future; all bullets; explain = changed perception + dream.)

**If you run dry:** buy time naturally — "Another thing I should mention is...", "Looking back,...", "What matters most to me is...". Never stop before the examiner stops you; a full 2 minutes with fillers beats 1 minute of perfection.

## 9.6 Part 3 Method: Opinion Architecture

Part 3 questions are abstract (*Why do people...? How has X changed? What are the advantages...? Should governments...?*). Use the **OREC frame**:

- **O**pinion: *I think... / In my view... / It depends...*
- **R**eason: *...because / ...since / The main reason is...*
- **E**xample: *For example/instance... / Take my city...*
- **C**oncession/contrast: *Having said that... / On the other hand... / Although some people argue...*

>>> "Should working hours be reduced?" (links to the course's Writing essay on automation — reuse ideas across modules!): "In my view, yes, at least in office jobs, because automation has made many routine tasks redundant, so long hours no longer mean higher output. For example, several European firms that moved to four-day weeks reported stable productivity and happier staff. Having said that, shorter hours only work if wages are protected — otherwise people simply take second jobs, which defeats the purpose." (Opinion + reason + example + concession in 4 sentences.)

**Part 3 survival kit:**

- Stalling elegantly: "That's an interesting question — I've never really thought about it, but I'd say..."
- Balancing: "On the one hand... on the other hand... on balance..."
- Speculating: "It's hard to predict, but... / ...is likely to... / There's a good chance that..."
- Comparing past/present: "In the past... whereas nowadays... / Compared with a decade ago..."

## 9.7 Pronunciation Training Programme

From the course's pronunciation lessons (Parts 2/6), adapted into drills:

### Individual sounds (L1 hotspots)

| Target | Drill pairs (say, record, compare) | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| p / b | park–bark, paper–baby, cup–cub | P: puff of air (hold tissue — it should move) |
| th / s / t | think–sink, three–tree, mouth–mouse | TH: tongue between teeth, air flows |
| f / v | fan–van, safe–save | V vibrates (touch your throat) |
| e / i | bed–bid, ten–tin, men–mean | E is open; I is short and tight |
| silent letters | design, resign, sign, foreign, calm | The course's list: learn the 50 commonest silent-letter words |

### Word stress (the fastest intelligibility win)

- 2-syllable nouns/adjectives stress first (*TAble, HAPpy*); verbs often second (*rePEAT, deCIDE*).
- -tion/-sion/-ic stress the syllable before (*phoTOgraphy, ecoNOmic*).
- Stress shifts meaning: *REcord* (noun) vs *reCORD* (verb); *PREsent* vs *preSENT*.
- Drill: mark stress on 20 new words weekly; mis-stressed words are the top cause of "cannot understand" moments.

### Sentence stress & intonation

- Stress content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives), glide over grammar words: "I WENT to the MARket to BUY some FISH."
- Rising intonation for lists and yes/no questions; falling for statements and wh-questions.
- Drill (shadowing): 10 min/day — play a 20-second native clip, speak along simultaneously, matching music before words.

> Record a 1-minute Part 2 answer today and re-record the same card monthly. Pronunciation progress is invisible day-to-day and dramatic month-to-month — recordings prove it.

## 9.8 Band 5 vs Band 8 — Same Card, Annotated

**Card:** *Describe a meal you enjoyed recently. You should say: where you had it, who you shared it with, what you ate — and explain why you enjoyed it.*

**Band 5 response (typical):** "I enjoyed... a meal... last week. I go to restaurant with my friend. We eat... pizza and... chicken. It is very delicious. I like it very much. The restaurant is big and... good. I want go again." *Examiner notes: FC — frequent pauses, short phrases; LR — very limited (delicious, good, big), repetition; GRA — tense errors (go→went, eat→ate, want go→want to go), only simple sentences; P — generally intelligible but flat. Overall 5.0.*

**Band 8 response (same facts):** "A meal that really stands out was last Friday, when an old school friend took me to a tiny Syrian restaurant downtown that I'd never noticed. We ordered a mixed grill to share — lamb kebabs, grilled chicken, mountains of flatbread — plus this incredible lentil soup to start. What made it special wasn't just the food, though that was superb; it was the whole atmosphere. The owner came over twice to check on us, the place smelled of charcoal and spices, and my friend and I ended up talking for three hours — we hadn't seen each other in two years. I suppose I enjoyed it because it combined everything I love: good company, authentic flavours, and that feeling of discovering a hidden gem in my own city." *Examiner notes: FC — smooth, natural hesitation only; LR — stands out, tiny, mountains of, superb, hidden gem; GRA — past narrative + relative clauses + cleft (What made it...) + nominalisation; P — expressive stress/intonation. Overall 8.0.*

*Same facts, different language. Record the Band 5 version of every card first — then deliberately upgrade it, feature by feature, into the Band 8 version. That transformation IS your study programme.*

## 9.9 Examiner's-Eye FAQ

**Do examiners mark down accents?** No — only intelligibility. A strong accent with clear sounds and correct stress scores 8+; a "native-like" mumble scores 5.

**Is it OK to lie (invent stories)?** Yes — examiners test language, not truth. But fluent lies need simple plots: adapt true stories rather than inventing complex ones mid-talk.

**What if I dry up in Part 2?** Use rescue phrases (*Another thing I remember is... / Looking back... / What I'll never forget is...*) and describe anything — the place, the people, your feelings. Silence hurts; imperfect speech doesn't.

**Should I use idioms?** Natural idioms (*hidden gem, recharge my batteries, the tip of the iceberg*) help LR — forced idioms (*raining cats and dogs* about exams) hurt. One or two per part, fitting the topic.

**Does grammar self-correction lose marks?** Occasional correction (*I went — I mean, I've been twice*) shows control and is neutral-to-positive. Constant restarting signals weak FC.

**How is Part 2 timed?** The examiner lets you speak 1–2 minutes, stops you at 2, and asks 1–2 rounding-off questions. Aim to still be talking (strongly) when stopped.

**Can I ask the examiner questions?** Clarification (*Could you repeat that?*) is fine. Conversation (*And you?*) is not — this is a test, not a chat.

## 9.10 Scoring Yourself (after every practice recording)

| Criterion | Ask | Band 6 sounds like | Band 7 sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| FC | Did I keep going? Link ideas? | Pauses to find language; basic linkers (and, but, because, so) | Long smooth stretches; some less common linkers |
| LR | Varied words? Right words? Paraphrase? | Enough for topics; repetition; errors in word choice | Less common items; collocations; successful paraphrase |
| GRA | Mix of sentences? Tenses right? | Simple + some complex; errors rarely block | Frequent error-free sentences; good complex range |
| P | Easy to follow? Stress right? | Generally clear; L1 accent; some mis-stress | Clear throughout; only rare lapses |

Give 5.0–9.0 per criterion with one evidence note each. Your Speaking band = the average. Fix the lowest criterion first — always.

## 9.11 Full Part 1 Mock Interview — Annotated (Band 8)

*E = examiner, C = candidate. Annotations in brackets.*

**E:** Good morning. Can you tell me your full name, please?
**C:** Good morning — I'm Rana Khalil. [Polite, clear, correct stress on surname.]

**E:** And where are you from, Rana?
**C:** I'm from Amman, the capital of Jordan — I was born there and I've lived there all my life. [Present perfect for continuing state — tense control from the first answer.]

**E:** Do you work or are you a student?
**C:** I'm actually doing both at the moment — I'm in my final year of pharmacy school, and I work part-time in a community pharmacy on weekends. It's exhausting but really useful. [Present continuous for temporary situation + concession.]

**E:** What do you like most about your studies?
**C:** Probably the practical side — last semester we started lab work with real formulations, which made everything we'd memorised suddenly make sense. [Past narrative embedded; "probably" hedges naturally.]

**E:** Let's talk about food. Who usually cooks in your family?
**C:** My mother, mostly — traditional Jordanian food, mansaf, maqluba, that kind of thing. I've recently started cooking myself, actually — simple stuff, pasta, salads — because I'll need to survive when I move abroad. [Tense range: present habits + present perfect + future purpose.]

**E:** Do you prefer eating at home or in restaurants?
**C:** Home food, definitely — it's healthier and, honestly, it just tastes like... home. But I do enjoy restaurants for the social side — last week my friends and I tried a new Italian place and ended up talking for hours. [Opinion + concession + past anecdote in 3 sentences.]

**E:** Has your diet changed in recent years?
**C:** Yes, quite a lot — I used to drink three coffees a day and skip breakfast, but since starting early hospital shifts I've switched to proper breakfasts and herbal tea. I feel much better for it. [Used to + since + present perfect + result — GRA showcase.]

**E:** Let's move on to travel. Do you like travelling?
**C:** I love it — though I haven't travelled nearly as much as I'd like. So far I've only been around the Middle East — Turkey, Egypt, the Emirates — but my dream is Japan. [Present perfect + limitation + future dream.]

**E:** Where did you go on your last holiday?
**C:** To Istanbul, last summer, with my sister. We spent five days just wandering — the Grand Bazaar, the Bosphorus ferry, all the clichés, but they deserve to be clichés! [Past simple narrative + humour + idiom ("clichés") used precisely.]

**E:** Would you like to live in another country in the future?
**C:** Yes — that's actually the plan. I'm hoping to do my master's in the UK, then decide. I'm excited but nervous, if I'm honest — I've never lived away from my family. [Conditional hope + honest concession — authentic, not memorised.]

*Overall: FC 8 (smooth, developed), LR 8 (mansaf/maqluba topic words, clichés, proper breakfasts), GRA 8 (present perfect/past/conditional all controlled), P 8 (clear, natural stress). Overall 8.0. Notice: every answer = direct + extend; no answer under 2 sentences or over 4; tenses always match the question.*

## 9.12 The Speaking Band Descriptors (working paraphrase)

| Band | Fluency & Coherence | Lexical Resource | Grammatical Range & Accuracy | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Fluent with only natural hesitation; fully coherent; topic development complete | Full flexibility and precision at all levels; idiomatic language natural | Full range of structures, always accurate | Effortless; L1 influence minimal; all features precise |
| 8 | Fluent with only occasional repetition/self-correction; coherent; wide connectives, well managed | Wide range, fluent and flexible; idiomatic items, rare slips | Wide range, flexibly used; majority of sentences error-free | Easy to understand; L1 influence has minimal effect; sustained facility |
| 7 | Some hesitation/repetition but keeps going; flexible connectives, some over/under-use | Sufficient range + some less common/idiomatic items; paraphrases effectively; occasional errors | Variety of structures; frequent error-free sentences; some errors persist | Generally clear; occasional lapses; all positive features present |
| 6 | Willing to speak at length but coherence sometimes lost; range of connectives but over/under-use | Wide enough for topics but lacks flexibility; paraphrases with mixed success; noticeable errors | Mix of simple/complex; errors rarely block; some complex sentences faulty | Generally intelligible; some mispronunciations but meaning rarely lost |
| 5 | Often cannot keep going; over-uses simple connectives; frequent breakdowns | Enough for familiar topics only; cannot paraphrase gaps; errors impede | Basic structures; errors frequent and sometimes impede | Often hard to follow; frequent mispronunciations; L1 strongly intrudes |

> Notice how Pronunciation Band 7 says "generally clear with occasional lapses" — perfection is NOT required. Clarity + word stress + sentence rhythm = Band 7 pronunciation for any accent.
