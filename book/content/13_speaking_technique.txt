# 17. Speaking Technique and Model Answers

This chapter turns the criteria of Chapter 16 into a working method, with the exact expressions and model answers taught and demonstrated in the course.

## Part 1 Technique

+ Answer in **3–4 sentences** (about 30 seconds) — enough to show language, not so long you ramble.
+ **Never** give one-word or two-word answers. Every answer needs a reason or a detail.
+ Use a **filler** to open instead of blurting the answer:

<<<
Fillers: As a matter of fact, … / To be honest, … / Frankly speaking, … / Actually, … / Well, …
>>>

+ **Follow the tense** of the question.
+ **Paraphrase** the question instead of repeating it.
+ Avoid starting every sentence with *and*. Use *also, in addition, what’s more, furthermore, moreover*.

### A weak answer and its upgrade

<<<
Q: Do you live in a flat or a house?
Weak: I live in a house. (one short sentence, no detail, no range)

Strong: As a matter of fact, I live in a house which includes two floors. It also has a small garden where I spend most of my free time reading. Honestly, I like my house a lot because it is very modern and cosy.
>>>

Notice the filler, the relative clauses (*which…, where…*), the linker (*also*), the reason (*because…*) and the richer vocabulary.

### Sample Part 1 exchanges (from the course)

<<<
Q: What do you like best about your family?
A: We are a family of five, and what I love most about my brother is that he always asks about my day and my feelings. We often go to a coffee shop together, eat sweets and talk about our lives, which makes us even closer.

Q: Do you like learning English?
A: Definitely. I want to be able to speak fluently in the future, and English is an international language that I need for my work, where I deal with multinational companies.

Q: What do you usually do at the weekend?
A: I usually spend my weekends relaxing after a busy week. I often meet my friends, and we go to a café or play football. Sometimes I simply stay home, read and focus on improving my English.
>>>

## Part 2 Technique (The Long Turn)

The examiner gives you a cue card like this:

<<<
Describe your favourite hobby.
You should say:
- what this hobby is
- why you enjoy it
- what benefits you can get from it
and explain why this hobby is important to you.
>>>

**The method:**

+ Use the **1 minute** to write **keywords only** — never full sentences. For the card above: *swimming — socialise — keep fit — relax*.
+ Cover **all the bullet points**, in order.
+ Speak for **1–2 minutes**. **Keep going until the examiner stops you** — it is always better to be stopped than to stop early.
+ Open with a **starter**:

<<<
Starters: I’d like to talk about … / Well, I’m going to talk about … / I guess the … I’d like to describe is …
>>>

+ If you run out of things to say, **generate more ideas** — the problem is never language, it is ideas. Ask yourself: *who? when? where? why? how did it feel? what happened next?*

### Model Part 2 answers

<<<
Topic: Describe your favourite hobby.

I guess the hobby I would like to talk about is reading. Actually, I have quite a few hobbies, but reading is the one I have stuck with the longest. I picked up this habit from my father, who used to buy me books throughout my childhood, and that sparked a real love of reading in me.

I read almost everywhere — on the bus on my way to work, during my breaks, and especially in my room at home. My room is really cosy and comfortable, with soft lighting, so when I feel stressed I go there, pick up a book and relax completely.

The benefits are enormous. Reading has enriched my vocabulary and improved my ability to communicate. It also opens my mind to completely new ideas and different points of view. That is why it is so important to me — it is not just entertainment, it has genuinely made me a better thinker and a better speaker.
>>>

<<<
Topic: Describe a teacher who influenced you.

I would like to talk about my physics teacher, whom I met when I was in high school. He was special to me because of the way he taught. He used to explain every single topic as if it were a piece of cake, always finding a much simpler way than the textbook did.

Another thing I admired was his fairness. He noticed the students who were struggling and gave them extra classes after school, without ever showing bias. He was demanding but always kind.

He influenced me so much because he changed the way I saw the subject. Physics was once my nightmare, but he made it fascinating. Thanks to him, I realised that a great teacher does not just teach a subject — they change the way you think. I still remember his words and his encouragement today.
>>>

## Part 3 Technique (The Discussion)

+ Speak **generally**, not personally. The examiner wants to hear about society, culture and trends.
+ Give an **opinion** and **justify** it.
+ Use generalising frames:

<<<
Generalising: I think that … / In my opinion, … / I believe … / It seems to me that … /
Generally speaking, … / Most people these days … / A lot of people nowadays … /
The majority of people in my country …
>>>

### Sample Part 3 exchange (from the course)

<<<
Q: What kind of gifts do people usually give each other in your culture?
A: I guess it depends on the relationship. If it is a relative or a family friend, people often bring sweets, chocolates or a cake as a sign of gratitude and love. But when close friends give gifts to each other, they usually try to find out what the person really needs or wants, and buy exactly that. So I would say the choice of gift really reflects how well the giver knows the receiver.
>>>

## A Question Bank to Practise

**Part 1 topics:** where you live · your city (and what you would change) · family · friends · work or studies · weekends · food · sport · music · films · travel · the internet · shopping · seasons and weather.

**Part 2 cue-card topics:** a hobby you enjoy · a teacher who influenced you · a person you admire · a gift you gave or received · a journey you remember · a place you like to visit · a book or film you enjoyed · a skill you would like to learn · something you do to stay healthy · a website you use often.

**Part 3 topics:** gifts and giving · festivals becoming more popular · the importance of teachers · technology in daily life · how people spend free time · health and lifestyle · cities of the future · the value of travel.

Now practise: pick one Part 2 card, write five keywords, and speak for two full minutes while recording. Do not stop. Then listen and count: how many linkers did you use? How many different tenses? Repeat until you can speak the full two minutes comfortably.
