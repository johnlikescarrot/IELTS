# 1. Welcome to IELTS

## What IELTS Is

**IELTS** (the *International English Language Testing System*) is the world’s most widely used English-language proficiency test. It is jointly owned by the **British Council**, **IDP: IELTS Australia** and **Cambridge Assessment English**, and it is accepted by universities, employers, immigration departments and professional bodies in most English-speaking countries.

The test measures your ability to use English in four skills — **Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking** — and reports each skill, plus an overall result, on a **nine-band scale** (Band 1 = non-user up to Band 9 = expert user). There is **no pass or fail**: your target band is set by the institution or organisation that requires the test.

## Academic or General Training?

There are **two modules**, and you must sit the right one.

[[table:Academic vs General Training]]
| | Academic | General Training |
| Who takes it | University entry (undergraduate/postgraduate), professional registration | Migration, secondary education, work experience, training |
| Reading texts | 3 long academic passages | 5 shorter everyday-life texts |
| Writing Task 1 | Describe a graph, chart, table, map or process (a report) | Write a letter |
| Writing Task 2 | Same essay task for both | Same essay task for both |
| Listening & Speaking | Identical for both modules | Identical for both modules |

> TIP: If your goal is **study** at university, take **Academic**. If your goal is **migration or work**, take **General Training** — but always check with the institution first, because requirements vary.

## The Test at a Glance

[[table:The four components]]
| Component | Time | Questions / Tasks | What you do |
| Listening | 30 min audio + 10 min transfer | 40 questions, 4 sections | Listen once, answer as you listen |
| Reading | 60 minutes | 40 questions, 3 sections | Read passages and answer |
| Writing | 60 minutes | 2 tasks | Task 1 (report/letter) + Task 2 (essay) |
| Speaking | 11–14 minutes | 3 parts | Talk with one examiner |

The **Listening, Reading and Writing** tests are taken in one sitting, in that order, with no break. The **Speaking** test may be taken on the same day, a few days before, or a few days after — depending on the availability of examiners — and you will be told the time by email or SMS (typically about 48 hours before the test).

## Paper or Computer?

The test content and scoring are **identical** on paper and on computer. The choice is only about how you deliver it.

[[table:Paper vs computer]]
| Feature | Paper test | Computer test |
| Answering | Write with a pencil on an answer sheet | Type on screen |
| Word count (Writing) | Count words manually | Automatic word counter |
| Editing | Erase and rewrite | Copy, cut, paste; highlight text (right-click) |
| Reading screen | One booklet, flip pages | Split screen: passage one side, questions the other |
| Search | Not applicable | **Ctrl+F is NOT allowed** |
| Results | ~13 days | ~3–5 days |

> REMEMBER: Even for the computer-delivered test, and even if your Speaking test is conducted by video link, **you must attend the test venue in person** — you cannot take the test at home.

## Before Test Day: Registration Essentials

- Book with a valid **identity document** (passport or national ID) and **bring the very same document** to the test. If you book with your ID, a passport will not be accepted, and vice versa. Candidates are turned away for this every single test day.
- **Arrive early** — about **one hour before** your scheduled time is advisable. Registration is a long procedure: identity check, photograph and fingerprinting.
- **If you arrive after the test has started, you will not be admitted.**
- Mobile phones, wallets, bags and watches must be left outside the test room. **Even a plain wristwatch is not allowed**; the time is shown on a screen in the room.
- If you have a **speech condition** (for example, stammering) or any other special requirement, **tell the centre when you book** — the Speaking examiner is informed and will take it into account.

## What Happens on Test Day

1. You register, have your photo and fingerprints taken, and are shown to your seat.
2. **Listening** (30 min audio + 10 min transfer), then **Reading** (60 min), then **Writing** (60 min).
3. Your **Speaking** interview, unless it is scheduled for another day.

> WARNING: The Reading test has **no transfer time**. You must write or type each answer directly onto the answer sheet as you go — otherwise you will score **zero** even if you knew every answer.

# 2. Understanding Scores

## The Nine-Band Scale

Each of the four skills is reported as a whole or half band from **0 to 9**.

[[table:Band scale summary]]
| Band | User | Meaning (short) |
| 9 | Expert | Fully operational command; accurate, fluent, complete understanding |
| 8 | Very good | Fully operational with only occasional unsystematic errors |
| 7 | Good | Operational command with occasional inaccuracies; handles complex language |
| 6 | Competent | Effective command with some inaccuracies; can use fairly complex language |
| 5 | Modest | Partial command; copes with overall meaning but makes many mistakes |
| 4 | Limited | Basic competence in familiar situations only |
| 3 | Extremely limited | Understands only general meaning in very familiar situations |
| 2 | Intermittent | Great difficulty understanding speech and writing |
| 1 | Non-user | Essentially no ability beyond a few isolated words |
| 0 | Did not attempt | No assessable information provided |

## How the Overall Band Is Calculated

Your **Overall Band Score** is the **average of the four component scores, rounded to the nearest half band**:

- Average ends in **.25** → rounded **up** to the next .5 (e.g. 6.25 → 6.5).
- Average ends in **.75** → rounded **up** to the next whole band (e.g. 6.75 → 7.0).
- Average ends in **.125** → rounded **down** to the previous whole band (e.g. 6.125 → 6.0).

**Example:** Listening 6.5 + Reading 6.5 + Writing 6.0 + Speaking 6.0 = 25.0 ÷ 4 = **6.25 → Overall 6.5**.

## Listening and Reading: Raw Score → Band

Listening and Reading each have **40 questions, one mark each**. The number of correct answers (your **raw score**) is converted to a band using a table. The tables below are the standard published conversions; **the exact boundaries vary slightly from one test paper to another**.

[[table:Listening band conversion (same for Academic & GT)]]
| Band | 9 | 8.5 | 8 | 7.5 | 7 | 6.5 | 6 | 5.5 | 5 | 4.5 |
| Correct answers | 39–40 | 37–38 | 35–36 | 32–34 | 30–31 | 26–29 | 23–25 | 18–22 | 16–17 | 13–15 |

[[table:Academic Reading band conversion]]
| Band | 9 | 8.5 | 8 | 7.5 | 7 | 6.5 | 6 | 5.5 | 5 | 4.5 |
| Correct answers | 39–40 | 37–38 | 35–36 | 33–34 | 30–32 | 27–29 | 23–26 | 19–22 | 15–18 | 13–14 |

[[table:General Training Reading band conversion]]
| Band | 9 | 8.5 | 8 | 7.5 | 7 | 6.5 | 6 | 5.5 | 5 | 4.5 |
| Correct answers | 40 | 39 | 37–38 | 36 | 34–35 | 32–33 | 30–31 | 27–29 | 23–26 | 19–22 |

> NOTE: General Training Reading texts are easier, so GT **requires more correct answers for the same band**. This is why GT and Academic candidates can earn the same band fairly. (The source course makes exactly this point: a high band in Academic needs slightly fewer raw marks than in GT, which compensates for the harder texts.)

## Writing and Speaking: Four Criteria Each

Writing and Speaking are marked by trained examiners against **four published criteria**, each worth 25% of the component score.

**Writing (Task 1)** — Task Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy.
**Writing (Task 2)** — Task Response, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy.
**Speaking** — Fluency & Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy, Pronunciation.

> IMPORTANT: Examiners do **not** mark your opinions, your ideas or your handwriting beauty. They mark **how well** you express yourself — accuracy, range, organisation and pronunciation. Never fear expressing a “wrong” opinion; there is no such thing in IELTS.

## How the Writing Score Is Weighted

Writing has two tasks, but they are **not** equal:

- **Task 1 = one third** of the Writing mark.
- **Task 2 = two thirds** of the Writing mark.

Your Writing band = **(Task 1 + 2 × Task 2) ÷ 3**, rounded to the nearest half band.

**Example:** Task 1 = Band 7.0, Task 2 = Band 8.0 → (7 + 16) ÷ 3 = 7.67 → rounded to **7.5**.

This is why you are strongly advised to spend about **20 minutes on Task 1 and 40 minutes on Task 2**, and why many teachers tell you to **write Task 2 first**.

## How Long Does It Take to Improve?

A widely used rule of thumb — repeated in the source course — is that **raising your overall band by half a band takes roughly 100 hours of focused study**. This is not discouraging; it is a planning tool. A candidate at Band 5.0 who wants Band 6.5 should plan for around 300 hours of work, spread over 2–4 months. Chapter 19 turns this into concrete weekly schedules.

Now practise: open any IELTS band-descriptor table online and read the Band 7 descriptions for each criterion — you will see that every technique in this book targets exactly those descriptors.
