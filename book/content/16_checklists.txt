# 20. Quick-Reference Checklists

Print these pages. Run through the right checklist the day before the test, and again during each component.

## Listening — Before and During

+ Read the **instructions and word limit** for every set of questions.
+ **Predict** each gap’s answer type before the audio plays.
+ Listen for **spelled-out** names and places; distinguish B/P, double letters.
+ Write numbers as **digits**; write 0 not O.
+ Watch for **distractors and self-corrections** (“…actually, no…”).
+ Answers come **in order**; move on instantly if you miss one — never look back while the recording plays.
+ Check **spelling** (a misspelled answer is wrong).
+ Transfer answers in the **10 minutes** — never leave the sheet blank.

## Reading — Before and During

+ **No transfer time**: answer directly onto the sheet as you go.
+ **Questions first**, keywords, synonyms, then the passage.
+ Divide 60 minutes: **20/20/20** (or 23/23/14 for a lower target).
+ Start with **Part 1**; never get stuck longer than ~1 minute per question.
+ **Guess** — never leave a blank.
+ Match the **question type** to the technique (T/F/NG vs Y/N/NG; headings; matching).
+ Keep to the **word limit** in completion questions.

## Writing Task 1 — Checklist

+ Know whether you write a **report** (Academic) or a **letter** (GT).
+ Paraphrase the prompt — **never copy** it.
+ **Academic:** introduction → **overview** → body 1 → body 2; **no conclusion**; support with **figures**; make **comparisons**; no opinions.
+ **GT:** correct **tone** throughout; salutation → purpose → three bullet points (in order, one paragraph each) → concluding sentence → closing.
+ ≥150 words (aim 160–180); finish in ~20 minutes.
+ Check grammar and spelling in the last minute.

## Writing Task 2 — Checklist

+ Identify the **essay type** (opinion / discussion / advantages / problems / double question).
+ Answer **every part** of the question.
+ 4–5 paragraphs; **thesis statement** in the introduction; **topic sentence** for each body paragraph; **conclusion** at the end.
+ One **central idea** per paragraph; link ideas with cohesive devices.
+ ≥250 words (aim 260–280); ~40 minutes.
+ **Never** copy the question; **never** go off-topic; no headings, bullet points or invented statistics.

## Speaking — Checklist

+ Arrive early with the **same ID** you booked with.
+ Part 1: 3–4 sentences per answer, paraphrase the question, use a filler, never one-word answers.
+ Part 2: write **keywords only**, cover all bullet points, speak until the examiner stops you.
+ Part 3: speak **generally**, give opinions with reasons.
+ Follow the **tense** of the question; mix sentence types; use linkers.
+ Pronounce clearly (watch B/P, -ed endings); accent does not matter.
+ Moderate body language, eye contact, dress smartly; don’t memorise answers.

## The Final Week

+ Day 7: one full timed Listening + Reading test. Mark and log every mistake.
+ Day 6: two Task 1 and two Task 2 essays, self-marked against the checklists.
+ Day 5: record a complete Speaking mock (Parts 1–3).
+ Day 4: re-read your weakest chapter(s); learn 30 new synonyms.
+ Day 3: one full Writing test under strict time.
+ Day 2: light review — checklists, band tables, trend language, linkers.
+ Day 1: prepare documents and route; relax; sleep early.

## The Ten Most Expensive Mistakes

1. Forgetting the Reading test has **no transfer time**.
2. Writing **more/fewer words** than the instruction allows in a completion answer.
3. **Copying** the question in Writing instead of paraphrasing.
4. Missing the **overview** in Task 1 or the **conclusion** in Task 2.
5. Answering only **half** of a two-part essay question.
6. Starting Reading with the **hardest** passage and exhausting yourself.
7. Getting **stuck** on one Reading/Listening question.
8. Giving **one-word** Speaking answers.
9. Bringing the **wrong ID** or arriving late.
10. Writing an IELTS essay **off-topic** (the “camel” mistake).

Now practise: run the “Final Week” plan for your own test date, substituting real dates for Day 7…Day 1.
