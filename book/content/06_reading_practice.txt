# 9. Reading Practice — Worked Passages

Use this chapter as a self-test. Read each set of questions, apply the four-step method, write your answers, then check the answer key and the reasoning.

## Practice 1 — The Thames Tunnel (True / False / Not Given)

**Background (as taught in the course):** the passage describes the Thames Tunnel, built under the River Thames in the nineteenth century. Key facts from the passage: it was the **first tunnel ever built under a river**; people at the time **believed** it was “the eighth wonder of the world”; **people came from far and wide to see the marvel**; it was the **age of sail and horse-drawn coaches**; the **aim** had been for the tunnel to be used by **wheeled vehicles to transport cargo** so that it would **bring in a profit**; and the site later became a place **selling souvenirs**.

**Questions.** Write TRUE, FALSE or NOT GIVEN.

+ The Thames Tunnel was the first tunnel ever built under a river.
+ The Thames Tunnel was the eighth wonder of the world.
+ People were drawn from all over to see the Thames Tunnel.
+ In those days people were able to travel by sea or by land.
+ The aim of the tunnel was to make a profit as a tourist attraction.
+ Replicas of the tunnel could be bought as souvenirs.

**Answer key.**

+ **TRUE** — “first … ever built under a river” = the same information.
+ **FALSE** — the passage says people *believed* it was the eighth wonder (an opinion); the statement states it as a fact.
+ **TRUE** — “drawn from all over” = “came from far and wide”.
+ **TRUE** — “travel by sea” = “the age of sail”; “travel by land” = “horse-drawn coaches”. (Do not rush to “not given”.)
+ **FALSE** — the passage says the aim was to be used by **wheeled vehicles to carry cargo** for profit; “tourist attraction” contradicts this.
+ **NOT GIVEN** — souvenirs are mentioned, but the passage does not say whether **replicas of the tunnel** were sold.

## Practice 2 — Handedness (Yes / No / Not Given)

**Background:** a passage about left-handedness and the scientists who studied it.

**Questions.** Write YES, NO or NOT GIVEN.

+ The study of twins shows that genetic determination is not the only factor in handedness.
+ Marc Dax’s report was widely accepted by other experts.
+ Dax based his findings on people who had language problems.
+ Left-handed individuals are thought to have more stable and successful relationships.

**Answer key.**

+ **YES** — one in five identical twins differs in handedness despite identical genes; genes are “not solely responsible”.
+ **NO** — “other experts showed little interest” contradicts “widely accepted”.
+ **NOT GIVEN** — the passage discusses the functional organisation of language, not subjects with language problems.
+ **NOT GIVEN** — no such claim is made anywhere.

## Practice 3 — Matching People to Findings (Handedness)

Match each scientist (A–C) to the correct finding (1–4). One finding is not used.

**Scientists:** A. Brenda Milner · B. Peter Hepper · C. Michael Corballis

**Findings:**

+ The origin of human speech lies in gestures.
+ Language is processed much more intensely in the left hemisphere.
+ The unborn baby’s preference for the faster-developing side influences brain development.
+ Handedness is fixed at birth and cannot change.

**Answer key:** 1–C · 2–A · 3–B · 4–not used.
