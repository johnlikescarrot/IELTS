# 18. Vocabulary Builder

Vocabulary is the single most transferable investment you can make: it feeds Listening, Reading, Writing **and** Speaking. This chapter gives you organised, high-yield banks. Learn a little every day, and always learn **words with their synonyms and collocations**, not in isolation.

## Idioms and Natural Expressions (for Speaking)

Use sparingly and only when they fit — a few natural idioms lift Lexical Resource; overuse sounds memorised.

+ *a piece of cake* — very easy
+ *to keep fit* — to stay healthy
+ *to break the ice* — to make people feel relaxed
+ *first impressions last* — appearance matters at the start
+ *out of the blue* — unexpectedly
+ *to put yourself in someone’s shoes* — to imagine their situation
+ *a mile a minute* — very fast
+ *to broaden your horizons* — to widen your experience
+ *to unwind / to recharge your batteries* — to relax
+ *to keep in mind* — to remember

## How to Learn Vocabulary Effectively

+ Learn words **in phrases** (collocations): *make a decision* (not “do a decision”), *do homework* (not “make homework”), *heavy traffic*, *strong coffee*, *a wide range of*.
+ Write each new word in a **sentence about yourself**.
+ Group words by **topic** (as above) and by **synonym sets**.
+ **Review** with spaced repetition: a word must be met and used several times before it is yours.
+ Read model answers aloud and **notice** the vocabulary; then write your own version using the same words.

> TIP: The source course repeatedly tells its students to read model answers for Speaking and Writing. Model answers do two jobs at once: they give you **ideas** (so you never sit silently in Part 2) and they give you **advanced language** to borrow.

Now practise: choose one topic block above, write a 150-word paragraph using at least eight of its words, then swap the paragraph with a partner (or a teacher) to check the collocations are natural.
